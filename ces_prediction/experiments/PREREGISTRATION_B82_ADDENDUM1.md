## 2. tau and its classes (registration section 3)

- **tau** of a D1 row t (ms) = (s(t) − s(j + 1)) × 1000, where j is B.75's S row of t (`jS_g` of `data/.b75/inputs/S.npz`: the last row before the most recent absent step (j, j + 1) of the block of t with j + 1 ≤ t; absent step = a step above 15 and below 45 ms inside a block). The stamps are those of the grid (float32 values as stored). tau is the same under every rule (it is a property of the row, not of the history). Rows with no such step (jS_g = −1) form the class "no notch before".
- **Class edges** with the 3 ms tolerance of every stamp comparison of B.75: up to 100 ms = tau ≤ 103 ms; above 100 and up to 300 ms = 103 < tau ≤ 303 ms; above 300 ms = tau > 303 ms. tau lies within float32 rounding of a multiple of 10 ms (every stamp is on the 10 ms lattice of its segment within 3 ms; B.78), so the tolerance moves no row; the readout of check 'tau' counts the rows within 1 ms of 103 and 303 ms.

## 3. c and its classes

- **c** = |a2(BES)| + |a2(ECEI)|, a2 being B.75's anchor scalars (columns 1 and 4 of `common75.anchor_scalars`) of the unit (fast channels standardised with the unit's fit discharges) under the rule (the anchor row of the rule's inputs).
- **Cut points** per unit and rule: `numpy.quantile` (linear) at 1/3 and 2/3 of the finite c over the unit's fit rows in the rule's set. Lowest third: c ≤ first cut; middle: first < c ≤ second; highest: c > second; "c not finite" otherwise (no row of either set, since every TX input is finite there; reported with zero rows).
- Under V1 (V2) a row is classified by the unit that holds its discharge in each re-partition (assignment), so a row's class of c can differ between re-partitions; the class counts are reported per re-partition. TN and TXN use the classes of the same unit and rule.
- Cached in `data/.b82/cclass.npz` (class of every test row, cut points, c of the test rows) and `data/.b82/tau.npz`.

## 4. The learners of part C (registration section 5)

- **Seven scalars of an array mean** (Mirnov, or one BES column), exactly as B.75 forms the BES and ECEI halves: each channel standardised with the mean and standard deviation of `Feats.stats` over all grid rows of the unit's fit discharges (two-pass, exactly rounded sums); the array mean by explicit sequential sums of the standardised channels, divided by their number; the four scalars of the present = `window_feats` (level, departure from the mean of the ten rows before, their sd, one-row increment) per file; a1 = m(anchor), a2 = m(t) − m(anchor) with m = `run_mean10` of the standardised mean; a3 = ln(m_raw(t) / m_raw(anchor)) from `run_mean10` of the unstandardised mean (float64 of the float32 values), where both are positive, otherwise undefined. Order: level, departure, sd, increment, a1, a2, a3.
- **TM**: the ten history scalars and the seven Mirnov scalars (17 columns). **TXM**: TX's 24 columns and the seven Mirnov scalars (31). **TBi, TBm, TBo**: the ten history scalars and the seven scalars of the BES column 05 (channels 0105, 0205, 0305), 06 (0106, 0206, 0306), 07 (0107, 0207, 0307) (17 columns each).
- **Which column is innermost.** By the name, the first two digits number the row of the array and the last two the radial column. TBi (inner) is column 05 and TBo (outer) column 07 on the assumption that the column number increases with the major radius. The files carry no channel positions, so this direction is not established from the data; if it is the other way round, TBi and TBo exchange their meaning, and the table names the columns by number beside the labels.
- **Undefined Mirnov a3.** Kept as a missing value (the regression's own handling), like the anchor-row scalars of TXo in B.81: the rows of TM and TXM are those of T0 and TX. The number of fit and test rows with a missing input is stored with every fit and reported.
- **TXs (swap).** Frame index f = `common78.frame_index` (10 ms frames from the first grid row of the block; a file's first and last rows have none); segment ordinal k = the rank of the row's block among the blocks of grid rows of its file. For each unit and rule, separately for the fit side and the test side, the discharges of the side are taken in increasing file index and each is given one donor, drawn with `numpy.random.default_rng([20261182, crc32("swap|<unit>|<rule>")])` (`integers(0, n)` over the sorted candidate list): the candidates are the other discharges of the same side whose first segment has at least as many frames as the recipient's first segment ("whose segment has that many frames"); when no other discharge of the side has that many (the recipient has the side's longest first segment), the candidates are all other discharges of the side. The donor row of row t is the grid row of the donor with the same segment ordinal and frame index. Its fourteen columns replace columns 10 to 23 of TX: the eight fast scalars of the donor row (`Feats.window` of the unit at the donor's grid index) and the six anchor scalars of the donor row with the donor row's own anchor under the rule (the anchor of any grid row by B.75's visibility rule, `common82.anchors_all`; on the D1 rows it equals B.75's anchor rows, verified in check 'swap'). Where the donor has no such row, the fourteen columns are missing; where the donor row has no visible value under the rule, its six anchor scalars are missing. The ten history columns stay. The donor grid rows and files are stored with the fit.

## 5. Part D

T0 and TX refitted with `random_state` s = 1 to 10 (everything else as B.75) for V4a, V4b and V1_r2_k0 to k4, rules D650 and S, stored as learners `T0_seed{s}`, `TX_seed{s}`. Increment of seed s = 1 − SSE(TX, s) / SSE(T0, s) on the unit's test rows (T0 and TX of the same seed paired). The registered increment uses the stored estimates of B.75 (seed 20261116). For V1 r2 also the pooled increment per seed (sums of squared errors over the five units' test rows, which are all rows of the set). Reported: the registered value, the ten values, their smallest, median, largest and standard deviation (ddof 1), and whether the sign changes among the ten or differs from the registered one.

## 6. Part E

Over the D1 discharges (the 635 files of `rows.npz`), grid rows = rows of `grid.npz` other than a file's first and last row. Grid rows per discharge and absent steps per discharge (steps above 15 and below 45 ms inside a block): 10th, 50th, 90th percentile (`numpy.percentile`, linear). Over the D1 rows: percentiles of the reported value (`y`) and of L5 (where defined). Share of grid rows without a usable T_i = share of grid rows of the D1 discharges whose value is missing in the cut, held-free grid (`ti` not finite). Beam: B.79's joined columns; sources NB1B, NB1C, NB2A, NB2B, NB2C (NB1A is never on); on = power above 0.1 MW; an empty power is zero; total power = the sum of the five powers of the row (B.79's Pt). Percentiles of the total power over grid rows of the D1 discharges with at least one source on, the 13 files of `data/.b79/checks/validity.json` left out. Shares of grid rows with 0, 1, ..., 5 sources on over all grid rows of the D1 discharges, and beside them without the 13 files.

## 7. Intervals and points

- **Draws.** As B.75 and B.81: 20,000 draws, 2.5 and 97.5 % quantiles; under V1 (V2) a re-partition (assignment) drawn for each draw first, then the units with at least one row in the cell resampled with replacement (multinomial counts); under V3, V4a, V4b the units of the scored rows. Units: shot clusters (cluster form, judged) or discharges (discharge form). Stream of a quantity: `[20261182, crc32(<name>)]`, the name being the statement id when the quantity is a statement and otherwise the cell name (below); in each stream the cluster-form draws are taken first, then the discharge-form draws.
- **Strata of part A.** A cell is design × rule × block (V1: all rows, and the block from 32312) × family (T0, TX, TE, TB on B.75's set; TN, TXN on B.79's set). Its units are the clusters (discharges) with at least one row in the cell; every class and every difference of two classes is formed on those draws, a class contributing the sums of its own rows of each drawn unit (zero for a unit without rows of the class). Thus the two classes of a difference share the draws. Cell names: `A|<design>|<rule>|<block>|<tau or c>|<class>|<comparison>` and `A|...|<difference>|<comparison>`. Reported differences: tau up to 100 ms minus above 300 ms; c highest third minus lowest third (A5 and A7 are those of V1 under S and D650).
- **Points.** V1, V2: mean over the three re-partitions (assignments) of the per-split value; V3: fifths 2 to 5 pooled; V4a, V4b: the test block. A skill on a class: 1 − SSE(A on the class) / SSE(R on the class). RMSE: mean over splits of the per-split RMSE.
- **Part B.** Per estimator (H and G of the rule's inputs; T0, TX of B.75 for D10, D650, S; O = the reported value of row t), on the rule's set: MAE; median, 75th and 90th percentile of |estimate − L5|; median of |estimate − L5| / L5 over rows with L5 above 100 eV; shares of rows with |estimate − L5| / L5 at most 0.10 and at most 0.20 over all rows of the set (L5 is at least 4.2 eV on both sets, so the ratio is defined on every row). Ratios TX over T0 of MAE, of the three percentiles and of the median relative error; differences TX minus T0 of the two shares. Per discharge with at least 50 scored rows in the cell: skill of TX over T0 on its rows (per split under V1, V2); the share with positive skill and the quartiles over discharges. Every quantity of part B has an interval: per draw, a mean or share is (counts @ per-unit sums) / (counts @ per-unit row numbers); a percentile is the quantile of the resampled rows, computed as a weighted quantile with the count of the row's unit as an integer weight; the discharge statistics weight each eligible discharge by the count of its unit (cluster or discharge). Cell stream `B|<design>|<rule>`; statements B1 to B8 on their own streams (on which only T0, TX and, for B6, the per-discharge skills are computed); the cell then carries the statement's value for that quantity, so that a quantity has one interval.
- **Weighted quantiles, fast and exact.** Percentile method: numpy's default (linear) on the resampled rows, reproduced exactly: virtual index h = T q + (1 − q) − 1 with T the total weight, the values at the integer positions floor(h) and floor(h) + 1 of the resampled sorted array, numpy's `_lerp`. The rows of a cell are sorted once; they are split into at most 400 contiguous chunks of the sorted order, the per-unit cumulative row counts at the chunk ends are formed once (units × chunks), and for a block of draws the cumulative weight at every chunk end is counts @ that matrix (exact in float64: integer sums far below 2^53). The chunk holding a position is found from it, and the position inside the chunk by a cumulative sum of the weights of its rows. Cost per draw: one row of a matrix product and one chunk, instead of a sort of 160,000 rows. Verified against the explicit computation (rows repeated by their unit's count, `numpy.quantile`) on 20 draws per form: before the checks on an input (c of V1_r2_k0, D650; `data/.b82/checks/wq_inputs.json`) and in the readout on the absolute and relative errors of TX (V1, D650, first re-partition), reported in the JSON (`wq_direct_check`); the agreement required is exact equality.
- **Part C.** Cell = design (V1, V3, V4a) × rule; comparisons TM/T0, TXM/T0, TXs/T0, TBi/T0, TBm/T0, TBo/T0, TX/T0, TX/TM, TXM/TX with intervals and the RMSE of every learner. Stream `C|<design>|<rule>|<comparison>`, or the statement id.
- **Judgement** (cluster interval): A1-A8, B5, C2, C3, C7 hold when the lower end is above zero; B6 when the lower end is above 0.5; B1, B2, B3, B4, B7, B8 when the upper end is below one; C1, C4, C5, C6 when the lower end is at most zero ("contains zero or lies below it"). "V4a" is the fit to 31991. "Expectation met": for "holds", met when the verdict holds; for "uncertain", not applicable.
- **Draws without a value** (a class with no row in every drawn unit) are left out of the quantiles and counted in the JSON (`draws_without_a_value`).

- All 520 fits were run once (two processes of two threads, about 4.5 min): part C 240 (TM, TXM, TXs, TBi, TBm, TBo; 20 units; D650 and S), part D 280 (T0 and TX, seeds 1 to 10; seven units; D650 and S). No fit was repeated or changed. The fit logs carry unit names, fit counts and times only.

Nothing was changed because of them.
- **Correction of section 7, before any readout.** The verification of the weighted quantile on the input c (not a registered check; no target or estimate) first gave a largest difference of 2.9e-15 against `numpy.quantile` on the resampled rows. Cause: numpy's default (linear) method computes the virtual index as (n − 1) q, not by the Hyndman-Fan formula that section 7 quotes (both are equal in exact arithmetic and differ in the last bit). The code now uses (n − 1) q; the verification was run again and gives 0.0 on 20 draws per form (and 0.0 on 30 synthetic sets with 20 draws each). Section 7's "virtual index h = T q + (1 − q) − 1" reads "h = (T − 1) q". No error, skill, share, percentile or interval of any estimator had been computed.
- What had been seen at that time besides the checks: row counts per unit and rule, the class counts of tau (section 1), and the stored pooled points of B.75, B.79 and B.81 (known numbers) as reproduced by check 'classes'.
