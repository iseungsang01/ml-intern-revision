## Applied paragraphs

The KSTAR chord studied here was selected by the data provider as the pedestal top ($R = 2.198$\,m). On it the CES value is weak in three ways: one frame in twelve carries no usable value, the values that exist scatter from frame to frame, and some are failed fits (\S\ref{sec:data}). Under the conventional beam-modulation analysis described for KSTAR, the value of a beam-on frame also rests on a background measured in frames without beam, including a later beam-off interval, so it cannot be completed at the moment the frame is taken~\citep{Lee2020TwoGaussian,Lee2026NeuralNetworkbased}; we did not measure when the values in our files became available and assign them no fixed delay. An estimate for monitoring or control has to be formed when the frame is taken, without the next value. We call such an estimate a \emph{nowcast}; the simplest one holds the last value.

Spectral analysis and nowcasting can therefore form complementary stages. NN-CES maps a measured CES spectrum to ion temperature and toroidal rotation without beam modulation~\citep{Lee2026NeuralNetworkbased}. Its reports could supply the upstream CES history for a temporal estimator. Our estimator uses already available CES reports and current and past BES and ECEI to estimate the current report before that report becomes available; it does not require the current raw CES spectrum. This is nowcasting rather than forecasting a future physical temperature. We evaluate both the current-frame report F and the future-derived level L5, which is an offline target defined below, not an input available online. The present evaluation uses conventional-analysis reports; coupling to NN-CES remains a possible implementation.

\emph{Relation to readers of the spectrum.} A possible modular system would use NN-CES~\citep{Lee2026NeuralNetworkbased} to analyse each acquired spectrum and update the CES history when its result becomes available. Between updates, the temporal estimator would combine that history with the available BES and ECEI to maintain a nowcast. Faster spectral analysis could refresh the anchors sooner, while also shortening the interval over which a nowcast is needed. The usefulness of the combined system would therefore have to be measured with its actual report timing and outputs. Here the history and targets come from conventional analysis: NN-CES outputs were not used to fit or evaluate the estimator, and no coupled pipeline was tested. The two methods address complementary estimation stages; their relative accuracy is not established by this study.
