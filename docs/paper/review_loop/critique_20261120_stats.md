## 2. Objections

### Objection 1. The gain belongs to part of the campaign and does not carry forward. A random discharge-level partition cannot show this, and it is the only design the paper reports.

1. **Statement.** The paper's subject is the fast-channel increment. With the same recipe (cap 80, early stopping, training-discharge statistics), trained on shots up to 31991 and scored on the 95 later discharges, the network's increment over its twin is +0.005 [-0.019, +0.028]; all sixteen pairings of the four network and four twin initialisations lie between -0.005 and +0.015. Its whole margin over the causal GP there (+0.107) is reached by the twin without any fast channel (+0.103). Its median absolute error is 57 to 65 eV by initialisation, against 56.3 eV for hold. **[V: C2]**
   - *Why is that so? Is it leakage between neighbouring shots under the random partition?* Partly, and little. 430 of the 640 gaps between consecutive delivered shots are 1 **[V: C3]**, and the folds are a plain permutation of discharges with no grouping **[V: `run_b27.py:115-121`]**, so nearly every test discharge has a neighbour in training. Natural experiment: each discharge is a test discharge in eight re-partitions with a random number of neighbours in training. Per additional training discharge within one shot number, ln(SSE network / SSE causal GP) falls by 0.0095 (cluster-robust se 0.0042, permutation p = 0.018; within three shot numbers 0.0058, p = 0.021). The twin falls by 0.0059 (p = 0.064). The ratio network / twin does not move (p = 0.41). **[V: C3; 4,799 units, 602 discharges]** Adjacency is worth 1 to 2 % of the squared error of both networks and nothing detectable of the increment. It is not the explanation.
     - *Why, then, is the increment gone out of time?* Because it was never a property of the campaign. Inside the authors' own random cross-validation it is three to four times smaller after shot 32000 (section 1, rows 3 to 5), and for the first stage its interval contains zero on shots 32002-32310 in all three re-partitions. **[V: C10]** Ten of the 86 shot clusters (G = 3) hold 0.79 of the first-stage reduction and 0.66 of the two-stage one; one cluster of 13 discharges (shots 31934-31948, 2.7 % of the rows) holds 0.22 and 0.20; the increment is negative in 28 and 25 of the 86. Without the ten largest clusters (469 to 479 discharges left) it is +0.017 and +0.052. **[V: C4]** The median statement fails the same way: the two-stage median error is 37.5-38.0 eV against 42.9 for hold up to shot 31991, 53.5-54.4 against 52.8 on shots 32002-32310 and 56.2-56.4 against 56.3 from 32312. The registered D1-5 (ratio 0.908 [0.887, 0.931]) is the first two thirds of the campaign. **[V: C10]**
       - *Why is the gain in those clusters?* It follows the kind of discharge. Per discharge, the increment ranks with the median T_i (Spearman +0.33 first stage, +0.40 two-stage) and with the mean beam emission (+0.29) **[V: C4]**; it is absent in the lowest fifth of BES level (+0.001 and +0.037) **[V: C6]** and in the coldest third of discharges (authors: `PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` 4.33 D2; `b56_kfx.md:74`). After shot 32000 the mix is different: hold's median error is 52.8 to 56.3 eV against 42.9 before, and the largest later cluster (G = 10: shots 32054-32194, 80 discharges) has a median T_i of 255 eV and 23 % of its frames without a value. **[V: C4, C10]** What those sessions were is not in the files and the paper does not say. **[J]**
         - *Why does the paper's interval not show any of this?* Three reasons. (a) The skill is a ratio of sums of squared errors, so hot, variable sessions set it: the ten discharges with the largest reduction hold 0.34 of the first-stage reduction and 0.26 of the two-stage one, the increment is positive in 403 and 441 of 635 discharges, and the Kish-type number (sum d)^2 / sum d^2 of the per-discharge reductions is 34 and 77. **[V: C4]** (b) The bootstrap resamples discharges as exchangeable; their contributions are correlated within clusters. Resampling clusters widens the interval of the two-stage increment from [+0.080, +0.115] to [+0.065, +0.135] (G = 3) and [+0.057, +0.138] (G = 10), about twice as wide; the first-stage one from [+0.036, +0.069] to [+0.025, +0.082]. **[V: C3]** (With eleven clusters, G = 30, the percentile interval is not wider; eleven unequal clusters do not carry a bootstrap.) (c) r2, r3, r4 and the eight re-partitions of B.39 are draws of one exchangeable scheme. Their agreement (+0.095, +0.097, +0.098) measures the repeatability of the scheme, not generalisation. **[J]**
           - *Why does the network lose even the little that later discharges carry, when the trees keep +0.02 to +0.04 and KFX +0.027?* The network reads the fast channels standardised with training-discharge statistics **[V: `seq_data.py:250-261`]**; the authors' record says BES drifts by 1.22 sigma across the campaign (`experiments/README.md`, row `campaign/`; not re-measured here); and its early stopping is on a validation block that is itself later: best epochs 15, 25, 19, 24 against 38, 26, 47, 29 for the twin **[V: `data/.b69_runs.json`]**. A level-standardised input does not transfer; a split on a departure from a ten-row mean, or a gain on a log change, transfers better. **[J]**
   - **THE ROOT.** The validation treats discharges as exchangeable draws of one population. They are not: the effect lives in particular sessions of the first two thirds of one campaign, and the only chronological test of the network shows no fast-channel increment.
   - **What settles it.** [existing data, CPU] Every main number by chronological block and by shot cluster; rolling-origin and cluster-blocked fits of every estimator that needs no network (trees: done here; KFX: minutes). [needs training, GPU] The fifteen remaining inner runs of B.69, then C1 to C4 as registered; then a five-fold forward-chaining run of network and twin, about 40 runs. [needs data that do not exist here] A second campaign.
   - **Honest claim if it is not settled.** "Under random discharge-level cross-validation of the 2022 campaign the increment is 0.05 (network) and 0.10 (two-stage). It is concentrated in discharges before shot 32000. On discharges later than those used for fitting it is 0.02 to 0.04 for a boosted regression and is not established for the recurrent network."
   - **Prediction, recorded before B.69 is finished. [J]** C3 near +0.03 to +0.05 (registered expectation +0.04 to +0.10). C4 fails: on those 95 discharges the two-stage median equals hold's even under the random partition.

### Objection 2. The object of the paper, a recurrent network that reads fast diagnostics, is not what produces the result. Conclusions read through it are properties of that learner.

2. **Statement.** An estimator with no network has the error of the paper's estimator. It is the causal GP plus B.64's boosted regression (default settings) of the GP's residual on 18 scalars: ten from the CES history, eight from the array means of BES and ECEI. Skill over the stored two-stage estimator: +0.000 [-0.013, +0.015], -0.004 [-0.014, +0.006], +0.003 [-0.008, +0.015] on r2, r3, r4. RMSE 175.0, 175.0, 174.9 eV against 175.0, 174.6, 175.1. Median absolute error 39.7 to 39.8 eV against 41.0 to 41.5. Over the offline GP +0.063. **[V: C7]** This is a plain five-fold cross-validation of a tree model on causal per-row inputs; it has no nested structure to leak through.
   - *Why can 18 scalars and default trees match 357,570 parameters and 120 inner GPU runs?* Because the first stage is a weak learner on this problem. A boosted correction on 15 hand-made inputs removes 9.2 % of its MSE **[V: `b70_d1.md:35`]**. Trees on the CES history alone are ahead of the twin network by +0.051 to +0.066, and of the two-stage twin by +0.009 to +0.016 (intervals above zero). **[V: C7]** The first stage's fast-channel adjustment is 30 % too large (slope of the twin's residual on the adjustment 0.70; 0.59 in its largest 2.5 %). **[V: C5; `b57_yardsticks.md:49`]**
     - *Why is it weak?* It is fitted to a squared error of which one row in a hundred holds about half (`PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` 4.10), stopped on a validation loss pooled over T_i and V_rot **[V: `train_seq.py:160-161, 293-300`]**, once per fold, at a width taken from a flat ladder of one recipe. **[J]** None of this was ever tuned against a non-recurrent learner, because the reference set holds none.
       - *Why does that matter, if the second stage repairs it?* Because every mechanism statement of the paper is measured with the first stage as the instrument. Three readings change with a second instrument. **[V: C7 for all three]**
         - "The network loses its gain when BES is one frame old: the information is in the BES value of the present frame" (`main_ppcf.tex:80-81`). Give the twin's second stage the eight fast inputs one row old: it gains +0.019 to +0.024 over the same second stage without them. Give them to history-only trees on the causal GP: +0.016 to +0.020. All six intervals are above zero. With present-frame inputs the two gains are +0.094 to +0.098 and +0.085 to +0.088. About a fifth of the information is there one frame late. The lagged network (L1, -0.006 [-0.025, +0.016], `b59_inputs.md:9`) did not find it. The sentence describes that network, not the data.
         - "A recurrent network that also reads BES, ECEI and Mirnov channels" as the carrier. A twin that reads no fast channel, with the eight array-mean inputs given only to its second stage, has the whole two-stage increment: +0.094 to +0.098 over the two-stage twin; the network-based form against it is -0.005, -0.004, +0.000, intervals containing zero. The network's reading of fifteen channels, of their radial structure and of the Mirnov coils adds nothing measurable to two array means.
         - What the network does add. With the same inputs on both sides, the network-based two stages over the trees: +0.014 [+0.007, +0.022], +0.010 [+0.002, +0.018], +0.004 [-0.006, +0.014]. On the last 95 discharges the stored two-stage estimator is ahead of the trees by 3 to 4 % in MSE (intervals below zero for the trees) and behind at the median (56.2-56.4 against 53.9-54.0 eV). **[V: C10]** That is the measured size of the network: about 1 % of the MSE.
           - *Why was this never seen?* The architecture was fixed before the question (it came out of an automatic search, `CLAUDE.md`), and every later batch varied its inputs, its training or what is stacked on it. "Trees on lags, with and without the BES summaries" was never an arm. The comparator that leads the abstract is also a weak history-only estimator: history-only trees beat the causal GP by +0.097 to +0.100, and the twin with a history-only second stage by +0.102 to +0.107 **[V: C7]**. Its noise grid stops at 0.1 of the variance of a 16-point window **[V: `baselines_interpolation.py:129-131, 188-192`]** on a series whose increments are close to white (lag-1 autocorrelation -0.42, `PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` section 2); the link between the two facts is **[J]**. Of the headline +0.177 over the causal GP, about +0.10 is the CES history used better and +0.08 to +0.10 on top of it is the fast channels.
   - **THE ROOT.** Nobody tested the assumption that the recurrent network is the right instrument. The reference set contains no learner of equal flexibility without recurrence, so the network's contribution and every readout taken through it are unidentified.
   - **What settles it.** [existing data, CPU] B.64's nested protocol with the first stage replaced by the causal GP and by the twin, with present-frame and one-row-old inputs; minutes. These rows belong in the main table.
   - **Honest claim if it is not settled.** "The gain is carried by eight scalar summaries of BES and ECEI (array means); about four fifths of it needs the present frame. A causal GP with a boosted regression has the estimator's MSE and a lower median error. The recurrent network adds about 1 %."

### Objection 3. What is verified is the prediction of the next reported number against that same number. Half of the gain or more is in one-frame moves that the next frame does not repeat. Nothing independent says the predicted part is ion temperature.

3. **Statement.** The verifying observation is the reported value of the frame. On consecutive frames its own frame-to-frame scatter (the authors' floor) is three quarters of the network's MSE: MSE over floor 1.331 **[V: `b57_yardsticks.md:17, 27`]**. In 97.4 % of the scored rows the last CES value is 10 ms old **[V: C10]**. What is scored is a one-frame-ahead prediction of a noisy series at frames where the measurement exists.
   - *Why is that a problem for a claim about fast diagnostics?* Because the predictor is not independent of the errors of the verifying observation: BES is the emission of the beam the CES measurement needs, at the same place, in the same frame. Skill measured against an observation whose error is shared with the predictor is inflated; this is standard in forecast verification (Ferro 2017, Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 143, 2665; Bowler 2008, Meteorol. Appl. 15, 199; neither is in `literature_map.md` 2.7). **[J]** The authors' defence is one number: the correction "is still present in the next, independently fitted frame" (rho_1 = 0.81).
     - *Does rho_1 carry that weight?* No. It is a ratio of covariances over all rows. It says where the correction points, not where the gain is. On consecutive triples (94.7 % of rows), take the rows whose reported value moves by more than 0.2 in ln (about 20 %) from the previous frame and comes at least half-way back in the next. They are 10.5 % of the triples. They hold 0.80 of the first-stage reduction over the twin and 0.57 of the two-stage one (0.64 to 0.88 and 0.48 to 0.65 for thresholds of 0.3 to 0.1), and 0.045 of the next-frame covariance that makes up rho_1. The rows with small moves hold 0.58 of that covariance and a negative share (-0.23) of the first-stage reduction. For the paper's estimator rho_1 is 0.68, not 0.81. **[V: C6]** The sentence of the abstract is true of the correction and false of the gain.
       - *Could one-frame moves be plasma?* They could be an event shorter than two frames. They could as well be the analysis answering the signal of that exposure. What the files allow: (i) the coupling is same-frame and positive. Rank correlation of the one-row change of ln BES with ln(value / previous value) +0.25; with the further change to the next frame -0.01. Of the moves that return, the down-moves come with BES down in 59 % of rows and the up-moves with BES up in 61 % (outside the rows after an absent frame). (ii) One-frame moves are about twice as frequent at low beam emission: 0.12 and 0.16 of the triples in the two lowest fifths of BES level, 0.06 and 0.09 in the two highest. (iii) Yet the gain is nil in the lowest fifth: +0.001 (first stage) and +0.037 (two-stage), against +0.08 to +0.12 and +0.13 to +0.17 in the three middle fifths. **[V: C6]** The same absence shows under the authors' weighting towards frames that resemble missing ones (+0.020 [-0.014, +0.049], `PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` 4.33 F4) and in the inclusive population (`b65_inclusive.md:10`). The abstract motivates the work with a chord on which "the signal is weak and values are missing" (`main_ppcf.tex:73-74`).
         - *Why can it not be settled here?* The files hold no per-frame fit error, photon count, spectrum, beam power, D-alpha or independent T_i (`PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` section 2, last row) **[V]**. Every analysis in the repository verifies against the reported value itself.
           - *And where the estimate would be needed?* When the CES updates stop, the network is not ahead of the causal GP after 50 ms and behind it after 100 ms **[V: `b71_scenarios.md:28-31`; `PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` 4.49]**. Size where it is scored: RMSE 9.1 eV below the same two stages without fast channels, median 1.8 eV below (41.2 against 43.0), on a floor of 136 eV RMS and a typical frame-to-frame scatter near 40 eV **[V: `b70_d1.md:20, 27, 38`; `b57_yardsticks.md:27`; `PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` 4.38]**.
   - **THE ROOT.** An assumption nobody tested: that a lower squared error against the reported value of the same frame is a better estimate of the ion temperature.
   - **What settles it.** [needs data that do not exist here] Per-frame fit uncertainties or spectra, or an independent T_i. [existing data, CPU] A partial test: score every causal estimate against a reference that does not contain frame t (the two-sided estimate from the neighbours, already computed as the offline GP) and against means over several frames; report the increment there, and the table of moves that return against moves that stay.
   - **Honest claim if it is not settled.** "The estimator predicts the value the CES analysis reports for the present frame. Half or more of what the fast channels add is in single-frame moves of that value. Whether those moves are ion temperature is not tested. There is no gain where the beam emission is weakest."

### Objection 5. Strata defined by the scored value cannot be used the way the paper uses them, in either direction.

5. **Statement.** D1-6 removes rows with a rule that uses the scored value and later rows **[V: `PREREGISTRATION_B70.md:79-84, 137-140`]**. The record reads the result as "the first stage's gain is in rows unlike their neighbours, and the two-stage estimator keeps both margins without them" (`PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md` 4.48).
   - *Why is that invalid?* Conditioning verification on the outcome sorts an estimator's hits into the flagged rows and its false alarms into the others. Any estimator that reacts to a predictor looks worthless on "normal" rows; any smoother looks better (the forecaster's dilemma: Lerch et al. 2017, Statist. Sci. 32, 106). **[J]**
     - *Is that what happens here?* Yes. I reproduce the flag as |ln value - centred 11-row median| > 0.207: 16.1 % flagged; on the unflagged rows network over twin -0.016, two-stage increment +0.069, two-stage over causal GP +0.215 (registered: 15.9 %, -0.015, +0.065, +0.207). Among the tenth of rows with the largest fast-channel adjustment, the flagged ones (3.3 % of all rows) give +0.165 and hold 0.83 of the whole reduction; the unflagged ones (6.7 %) give -0.109. A flag of the same form and threshold that uses only the past (last value against the median of the eleven rows before) splits the increment evenly: +0.058 and +0.050 (two-stage +0.102 and +0.097). The causal GP's skill over hold is +0.215 on flagged and +0.518 on unflagged rows: the stratum favours smoothing by construction. **[V: C5]**
       - *Why did it get in?* The rule is taken from profile fitting, where it weights points inside a fit. Used as a verification stratum it conditions on the verifying observation. The same holds for the "value rules" (value and hold within 150 to 2,500 eV, step below 1 keV), the 2 keV limit of B.52 and the statements on error percentiles. **[J]**
         - *What follows?* (a) D1-6 "holds" is not robustness to the outlier rule. (b) The first stage's zero on unflagged rows is not evidence of artefacts either. (c) Two readings in the record contradict each other because each conditions on a different function of the value: "the increment belongs to plain rows" (+0.092 on rows passing the value rules, `PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md:624`) and "the first stage's gain is in rows unlike their neighbours" (`PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE.md:866`). Neither is a finding. **[V of the two texts]** My own table of moves that return against moves that stay (objection 3) conditions on the outcome too; I use it only to compare two outcome-defined classes with each other.
           - *What is valid?* Strata built from predictors or from the estimates. Three, done here: by the size of the one-row change of ln BES (increment in every bin: +0.03 to +0.08 first stage, +0.08 to +0.13 two-stage; two fifths of the two-stage reduction sit in the three quarters of rows where BES changes by less than 6 %); by BES level (nothing in the lowest fifth); by the size of the adjustment (in the half of rows where the network moves the estimate least: first stage +0.004, two-stage +0.041, so that part of the two-stage increment does not come through the first stage). **[V: C5, C6]**
   - **THE ROOT.** Verification strata conditioned on the verifying observation.
   - **What settles it.** [existing data, CPU] Replace every outcome-defined stratum by predictor-defined ones, and report hits and false alarms and the slope of the residual on the adjustment.
   - **Honest claim.** "Of the tenth of frames in which the fast channels move the estimate most, one third are frames whose reported value departs from its neighbours (MSE 16.5 % below the twin's) and two thirds are not (10.9 % above)."

## 3. Checked and dropped

Each of these was a candidate objection. The check is given so that nobody spends time on it again.

- **The twin's per-discharge standardisation.** It cannot matter. The flag changes only the fifteen fast columns (`seq_data.py:250-261`), and the twin slices them off before both encoders (`model_seq_v2h.py:35-40`). **[V]**
- **A collapsed twin.** On the four splits two twins stopped at epochs 5 and 6 (`b39_primary.md:32, 34`). In the fifteen D1 folds the twin's best epoch is 19 to 80 (`b59_inputs.md:64-66`). **[V]**
- **Leakage in the second stage.** None found. The window inputs use rows before t (`run_b33.py:32-44`), the statistics exclude the test files (`stage2_b64.py:303`), the fit rows are the out-of-fold rows of non-test discharges (`stage2_b64.py:310-326`). The outer validation discharges, on which the outer network was stopped, are among the fit rows: a mismatch, not a leak. **[V]**
- **Unequal inputs of the two second stages.** Giving both arms five window inputs of the CES history leaves the increment at +0.082 to +0.096. **[V: C7]**
- **Leakage in the first stage.** None found. History inputs use rows before t (`seq_data.py:295-311`), statistics come from training files (`train_seq.py:227-228`), the LSTM is one-directional (`model_seq_v2.py:54-56`). **[V]**
- **Adjacent shots in training.** Detectable and small (objection 1, first level). **[V: C3]**
- **One initialisation per fold.** For the pooled reading the point is a mean over fifteen trainings and the spread between re-partitions is 0.015 (first stage) and 0.003 (two-stage) **[V: C1]**. It remains fatal for statements on a single split, as the authors' own S1 and S2 showed.

## 4. Verdict

**The strongest reason to say "this result is not real".** After shot 32000, a third of the campaign and the only part that can be scored out of time, the fast-channel increment of the network is +0.005 [-0.019, +0.028] when it is trained on earlier discharges, and +0.01 to +0.03 even when discharges of the same weeks are in training; the estimator's median error is not below hold's there. The pooled +0.05 and +0.10 are the first two thirds of one campaign, and within them ten shot clusters.

**The strongest reason to say "it does not matter".** The fast channels buy 9 eV of RMSE and 1.8 eV at the median, against a reported value whose own frame-to-frame scatter is 136 eV RMS. Half or more of that is in one-frame moves that the next frame does not repeat. There is none where the beam emission is weakest, none in the population that keeps values above 3 keV, and no advantage over a causal GP once CES has been absent for 50 ms. A causal GP with default boosted trees on 18 scalars does the same without the network.

**The analysis to demand first.** A chronological and cluster-blocked validation of the complete estimator with the no-network control beside it, every number by block. In order: today on a CPU, the tables of section 1 for KFX and for B.64's protocol with the causal GP as first stage; on the GPU, the fifteen inner runs of B.69 and its four statements as registered; then a forward-chaining five-fold run of network and twin.

**A defensible version of the claim.**

> On one edge chord of KSTAR in the 2022 campaign, summaries of the beam emission and of the electron-cyclotron emission of the present 10 ms frame lower the mean-squared error of a causal estimate of the C VI temperature that the CES analysis reports for that frame, relative to estimators that read the CES history alone. Under random discharge-level cross-validation the reduction is about 9 % of the MSE; with whole shot clusters held out, 7 to 8 %; fitted on earlier discharges only, 2 to 4 % on later ones, and for the recurrent network not separable from zero. The reduction sits in hot discharges with bright beam emission before shot 32000, half or more of it in single-frame moves of the reported value. It is absent at low beam emission and when values above 3 keV are kept. A recurrent network is not required: a causal Gaussian process with a boosted regression on 18 scalar inputs has the same error. The study is exploratory: every choice was made on the same 639 discharges, and whether the predicted part is ion temperature was not tested.

## Appendix B. Limits of these checks

- The chronological reading of the network (C2) is one block of 95 discharges with four initialisations, and it is the first stage. It says nothing yet about the two-stage estimator. It was not registered.
- The random cross-validation gives +0.026 to +0.034 for the first stage on those same discharges. The fall to +0.005 therefore mixes two things: later discharges carry less, and the network transfers poorly. The trees separate them (+0.036 to +0.040 at random against +0.023 and +0.037 out of time); for the network that needs training.
- Shot clusters are a proxy for sessions. A day boundary without a gap in shot numbers is not seen; a gap inside a day is.
- The refits of the network-based second stage (C7, variants built on the stored network or twin) use, as fit rows for fold k, the out-of-fold estimates of the other outer folds, whose networks had fold k's discharges in training. This is not B.64's nested design. The calibration runs come out slightly below the stored values, not above. The variants without a network have no such path.
- The variants of C7 were fixed in the scripts before each run. The three without a network were added after the first run on r2 and then run once on r2, r3 and r4. No other specification was tried. Rolling origin confounds "earlier only" with "fewer fit discharges" for the early folds.
- The table of moves that return against moves that stay conditions on the scored value and on the next one. It compares two outcome-defined classes with each other and supports no statement about either against the unconditioned rows.
- All of this is on the cut population and on T_i.
