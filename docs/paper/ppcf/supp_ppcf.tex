\paragraph{Plasma and diagnostics.} Table~\ref{tab:plasma} collects what is published on
the device, on the discharges of this data set and on the diagnostics, and marks what is
not published. The chord at $R = 2.198$\,m was selected by the data provider as the
pedestal top; in other campaigns the last closed flux surface lay near $R \approx 2.25$\,m
in H-mode~\citep{Ko2015IonTemperature}, the charge-exchange channels nearest the pedestal
top at $R \approx 2.19$ to 2.20\,m~\citep{Kim2020PedestalElectron}, a channel at
$R = 2.192$\,m is described as near the pedestal top~\citep{Lee2026NeuralNetworkbased},
and in an inter-ELM profile of 2014 the \Ti{} pedestal top is at $R \approx 2.22$ to
2.23\,m (read from figure~2 of~\citep{Ko2016CharacteristicsToroidal}): the pedestal-top
position differs between published discharges ($R \approx 2.19$ to 2.23\,m).
In the published equilibrium of a 2022 discharge of this set (32092) the midplane
separatrix lies at $R \approx 2.24$\,m, the edge charge-exchange channels span
$R \approx 2.18$ to 2.25\,m about 4\,mm apart, and the pedestal tops of \Ti{} and the
rotation lie at normalized flux 0.95 to 0.97 (read from figures~1(c) and~3
of~\citep{Lee2026SpatiotemporalStructure}); the \Ti{} pedestal top of the H-mode phase of
31921 is at $r/a \approx 0.97$~\citep{Na2026FireMode}, and the beam-emission channel
BES\_0206 of the block used here is printed at normalized flux radius about 0.96 in
figure~2(e) of~\citep{Heo2025ExperimentalIdentification}. $R = 2.198$\,m is thus about
4\,cm inside that separatrix; no publication gives the pedestal-top position of the 2022
discharges in metres. These locations are given in different coordinates (normalized
flux, $r/a$, the normalized flux radius of a BES mapping) and for different discharges: 4\,cm
inside the separatrix is $r/a \approx 0.92$ if the separatrix is at $r/a = 1$ with
$a = 0.5$\,m, below the 0.94 to 0.97 printed in the papers; they are not reconciled here.

\paragraph{Where the fast channels are.} The files describe the BES and ECEI channels as
lying within about 1.5\,cm of the chord. The published geometry gives the following
(Table~\ref{tab:plasma}). The ECEI channels GT1103 to GT1403 belong to the transmitted-beam
array of the first ECEI system: the first two digits of a name are the vertical index
(01 to 24) and the last two the radial index (01 to 08), with radial channel 1 outermost
(the facility's data user guide~\citep{KFE2026DiagnosticsDataUserGuide}), and a channel's
frequency is $f = \mathrm{LO} + 2.6 + 0.9\,(n_r - 1)$\,GHz, placed at the cold
second-harmonic resonance (the public analysis codes~\citep{fluctana,syndia}). The four
channels are therefore vertically adjacent rows of radial column 3, about 2\,cm apart
around the midplane. In a published figure of 32092 that column is at $R \approx 2.19$\,m
(read from figure~1(b) of~\citep{Lee2026SpatiotemporalStructure}); between discharges the
position is uncertain by 2 to 3\,cm, because the local-oscillator frequency and the field
of each discharge are not published, plus about 1\,cm of mapping
error~\citep{Yun2011TwoDimensionalVisualization}. The BES names are BES\_[vertical 01 to
04][radial 01 to 16] on a $4\times16$ array of about 1\,cm pitch viewing the first beam
line, movable from discharge to discharge~\citep{KFE2026DiagnosticsDataUserGuide}. In the
published view of 31923 the array spans $R \approx 2.12$ to 2.34\,m (read from figure~1
of~\citep{Heo2025ExperimentalIdentification}), so columns 5 to 7 are at $R \approx 2.16$ to
2.21\,m if the column number increases outward, as printed for the earlier
array~\citep{Nam2014EdgeElectron} (inferred for this one). The charge-exchange channel
table of the facility's user guide gives, from the 2022 campaign on, channel 18 at
$R = 2198$\,mm, the chord of the files, with neighbours at 2192 and 2203\,mm and the
outermost channel at 2269\,mm~\citep{KFE2026DiagnosticsDataUserGuide}; with the published
separatrix near 2.24\,m in one discharge, the outer channels lie outside it. KSTAR raw data with CES, BES or ECEI
signals are not openly available: the one public KSTAR file with one of these diagnostics
is a 45\,ms de-identified 64-channel BES example~\citep{Joung2026PhysicsInformedPreprocessing},
and the paper that released it offers its data only on request to the corresponding
author~\citep{Joung2026ExplainableTokamakagnostic}.

\paragraph{What a row holds.} For twelve discharges the raw BES, ECEI and Mirnov
signals (0.5 to 2\,MHz) were delivered as well, and setting them against the 10\,ms
files shows how the files were assembled. Every fast-channel value of a row equals a
single raw sample within one raw step of the time stamp of the row (BES on 6{,}502 of
6{,}502 rows of the delivered windows, ECEI on 6{,}484 of 6{,}484; which step,
counted on 6{,}490 of the BES rows by a later readout with its own window edges, is given
in \S\ref{sec:rows}): the fast channels are snapshots, not averages over the CES
frame. 99.37\% of the steps between rows are 10\,ms. A step of 20, 30 or 40\,ms is
one, two or three absent frames (1{,}637 absent frames in 1{,}414 steps), and each of
the 45 such steps inside the raw deliveries coincides with a beam blip: the heating
beam is interrupted (for about 10\,ms when one frame is absent) and the frames exposed
without beam are left out. The converse does not always hold
(\S\ref{sec:bursts}: two interruptions of 23 and 26\,ms that removed no frame).
The files therefore record the blips, though not the beam power. In the raw stream the beam
is off from 1.7\,ms before an absent stamp to 8.8\,ms after it, which places the
frame: the time stamp of a row lies within about 2\,ms of the start of its 10\,ms
frame, so an estimate formed at the stamp uses fast-channel samples taken no later than
2\,ms into the frame it estimates and CES values of frames that had ended by then. The
published values conflict: 10\,ms frames with a 5\,ms
average~\citep{Ko2016CharacteristicsToroidal} or a 5\,ms exposure~\citep{Lee2020TwoGaussian},
and a ``10\,ms integration time''~\citep{Jang2021DevelopmentUltrafast}; none states the
2022 setting, so the placing of the frame relative to the stamp is
inferred from the beam interruptions. The published analysis subtracts a background
interpolated between the two neighbouring beam-off frames~\citep{Lee2020TwoGaussian}. Values that repeat the
previous one to the last digit are held values, not measurements (one \Ti{} row in
226{,}991; more than half of the rotation values), and are removed everywhere.

\paragraph{What the target is.} The quantity estimated is the ion temperature that
the KSTAR CES analysis reports for the chord: the temperature of C$^{6+}$ ions from
the Doppler width of the C\,VI ($n = 8 \to 7$, 529\,nm) charge-exchange line excited
by the heating beam, the beam being modulated for the
background~\citep{Ko2010KstarCesSystem,Ko2010ChargeExchange}, which is taken from frames
in which the beam is off~\citep{Ko2015IonTemperature,Lee2016ValidationToroidal,KFE2026DiagnosticsDataUserGuide},
one value per 10\,ms frame~\citep{Kim2024KineticProfile}. Whether that value equals the main-ion
temperature is a property of the diagnostic and is not tested here. Five things are
known. (i) At a pedestal top the impurity and the main-ion temperature are close but
not identical: at ASDEX Upgrade, in a discharge with raised electron heating, the
deuterium temperature was about 100\,eV above the impurity temperature before, and 50 to
60\,eV after, extended corrections, and using the impurity temperature in edge ion
heat-transport analysis was judged generally valid~\citep{CanoMegias2025MultiIon};
larger differences are reported at the separatrix~\citep{Haskey2018MainIon}, and
cross-section effects on the apparent width grow with the ion temperature and are small
at pedestal-top temperatures~\citep{vonHellermann1995CrossSection}. An offset that
is constant in time, or a constant factor, changes no skill of this paper, because
every estimator is scored against the same reported values; a bias that varies within
a discharge would. (ii) Outliers in KSTAR CES temperature profiles tend to come from
poor fits of the spectrum~\citep{Kim2024KineticProfile}. Here 1{,}197 \Ti{} rows
(0.53\%) exceed 3\,keV; they are isolated, and in a registered test they are 3.18
$[2.49, 4.04]$ times as frequent in the lowest fifth of the beam-emission level as in
the highest, as failed fits of a weak signal are. They are treated as missing for
every estimator. (iii) The files carry no per-frame fit error. The facility's database
holds the values as automatically calculated and uploaded, described as for preliminary
analysis, and has nodes for error bars~\citep{KFE2026DiagnosticsDataUserGuide}; they did
not come with the files. From the series
itself, the part of the reported value that is independent from frame to frame is
39.1 to 42.8\,eV on a typical row (a robust lower and upper estimate from triples of
consecutive rows; computed within thirds of the level and divided by the median level of
the third, 5.5 to 9.5\% of
the level), and it grows with the level (22.5, 33.4 and 90.7\,eV in the lowest,
middle and highest third). Two registered checks of this estimate failed (it is below
the reported fit error on MAST, and it does not fall with the beam-emission level as
photon noise would), so whether the component is fit noise or a fluctuation faster
than a frame is not decided by the files. This typical scatter of a row and the
mean-square floor of \S\ref{sec:yardsticks} (\FloorRms\,eV root mean square) are the
two noise quantities of the paper: the first is a robust value on a typical row, the
second a mean square over the rows between two scored frames, which a few large
excursions dominate. Published
values for other analyses or other discharges, not fit errors of the rows used here, are
of similar size: a median uncertainty of 0.080\,keV for \Ti{} and 3.84\,km/s for the
rotation over all channels and times of a 2022 discharge (32364), quoted in a Bayesian
profile-fitting paper~\citep{Kim2026AutomatedOutlier} for the values of the
neural-network analysis~\citep{Lee2026NeuralNetworkbased}, error bars of about 0.10 to 0.14\,keV at $r/a$
0.85 to 0.97 in the H-mode of 31921 (read from figure~10(a) of~\citep{Na2026FireMode};
single frame or average not stated), and a scatter of about 0.05\,keV at normalized
flux radius 0.94 in 31923 (read from figure~2(d) of~\citep{Heo2025ExperimentalIdentification});
in an inter-ELM profile of 2014 the error bars are about 15 to 25\,eV up to
$R \approx 2.25$\,m and grow to 70 to 350\,eV beyond 2.26\,m (read from figure~2
of~\citep{Ko2016CharacteristicsToroidal}).
(iv) The reported value follows the plasma
as seen by a diagnostic that uses neither the beam nor the spectrometer: the changes
of the 200\,ms means of \Ti{} and of the ECEI level from one block to the next have a
median rank correlation of $+0.268$ per discharge against $+0.003$ when one series is
shifted in time (difference $+0.265$ $[+0.126, +0.495]$ on the 15 discharges that
meet the registered requirement; $+0.328$ $[+0.275, +0.370]$ on 400 discharges with a
weaker requirement, read after the fact). A correlation of this size means that most
of the 200\,ms changes of the two are not shared, and it bounds no bias. (v) A bias
that changes when the analysis refreshes its background at a beam-off frame was
looked for in a registered test and is not seen. Across 1{,}188 isolated absent
frames in 460 discharges (those with at least ten measured \Ti{} rows in both 200\,ms
windows), the change of the 200\,ms mean of \Ti{} from before the
frame to 150\,ms after it has a spread 0.927 $[0.814, 1.075]$ of that between windows
the same distance apart with no absent frame between them (1{,}415 control stamps),
and its median differs from that of the controls by $-0.0017$ $[-0.0123, +0.0096]$
in the logarithm. An excess spread at a beam-off frame is thereby bounded at 5.17\%
of the level (7.19\% with controls from the same discharges only). The frame-to-frame
component 1.0 to 2.0\,s after a beam-off frame is 1.013 $[0.982, 1.051]$ times that
at 0.1 to 0.5\,s (292 discharges). A bias that varies in another way is not excluded.

\section{The second stage and the two-stage estimator}
\label{sec:twostage}

This section gives the protocol of the second stage of \S\ref{sec:methods:est} and its
readouts on the folds of three re-partitions of the pooled cross-validation (r2 to r4, 15
outer folds; \Ti{}, values above 3\,keV excluded). Six statements, the rule for the main
text and numerical expectations were registered before any run.

\paragraph{Inputs.} Fifteen quantities known at row $t$. From the fast channels, each
standardized with the mean and standard deviation of the training discharges of the fold:
the mean of the nine BES channels and the mean of the four ECEI channels, each as four
summaries (the value at $t$, its departure from the mean of the ten rows before $t$, the
standard deviation of those ten rows, and the change from row $t-1$). From the CES history
and the first stage: the time step in rows, the age of the last CES value, that value
(hold), the first-stage estimate minus hold, the first-stage estimate minus the causal GP,
and the absolute values of these two differences. No input is computed from the Mirnov
channels; the split was checked by varying the BES, ECEI and Mirnov columns of one
discharge in turn. The second stage of the two-stage twin reads the seven quantities of the
second group, computed from the twin's estimate.

\paragraph{Model.} A histogram-based gradient-boosted regression
(scikit-learn~\citep{Pedregosa2011ScikitLearn}, its default settings: squared error,
learning rate 0.1, at most 100 trees of at most 31 leaves, at least 20 rows per leaf, and
the library's early stopping on a tenth of the fitting rows). Model and settings are those
of the earlier second stage on the four file-level splits (\S\ref{sec:character}); nothing
was tuned for this batch. Its target is the residual of the first stage in eV (the reported
value minus the first-stage estimate); the two-stage estimate is the first-stage estimate
plus the predicted residual.

\paragraph{Fitting on discharges the network has not seen.} The errors of a network on its
own training discharges are smaller than on new discharges, so a second stage fitted there
would learn the wrong errors. For each outer fold the training discharges were divided into
four inner folds (seeded by the re-partition and the fold); the network and the twin were
each trained on three of them, with the outer fold's validation discharges for early
stopping and every other setting as in the pooled cross-validation, and estimated the
fourth: 120 trainings (15 folds, four inner folds, two networks). The stored outer-fold
networks of the pooled cross-validation give the estimates on the validation and test
discharges. The second stage of each fold and network is fitted on the out-of-fold rows of
its training discharges together with its validation discharges, on the rows where all its
inputs are present, and is applied once to the scored rows of its test discharges. No
discharge is in two of inner training, inner held-out, validation and test (checked for
every fold); four of the 120 trainings stopped at the epoch cap.

\paragraph{Missing inputs.} The window summaries are missing on the first ten rows of a
contiguous segment, a share \TwoFallback{} of the scored rows. On those rows both the
two-stage estimator and the two-stage twin keep their first-stage estimates (fixed in an
addendum before any run), so the two are compared with the same treatment of the same
rows. With the second stage applied on every row and the boosted model's own handling of
missing values, the six readouts change in the third decimal only (Table~\ref{tab:twopairs},
lower part; reported, not a statement).

\paragraph{Run time.} The second stage takes \TwoMicros\,$\mu$s per row for the network and
\TwoMicrosTwin\,$\mu$s for the twin, measured as prediction over many rows at once on a CPU.
A call for a single row costs more: in the earlier batch one step of the correction took
1.257\,ms at the median (\S\ref{sec:character}).

\paragraph{Checks and statements.} The stored errors of the pooled cross-validation reproduce
the registered increment of the network on r2 to r4 within the registered tolerance. All six
statements hold (Table~\ref{tab:twopairs}): the two-stage estimator has skill over the
network (T1) and over the causal GP, with a point value above that of the network over the
causal GP (T2), and over the two-stage twin (T3); the ratio of its median absolute error to
that of hold is below one (T4); its share within 20\% minus that of the causal GP is above
zero (T5); and the two-stage twin has skill over the twin (T6). By the registered rule (T1,
T2 and T4 met) the two-stage estimator is the estimator of the main text and T3 its
fast-channel increment; these statements rest on three re-partitions, the primary
statements on the network on eight. Table~\ref{tab:twothirds} gives the thirds of the
discharges by median \Ti{} (reported). \emph{Limits.} One initialisation per network; the
settings were chosen on single splits whose discharges overlap these; the second stage
reads the inputs that the network reads, so it adds no information, only a different use of
it.

\section{The five-parameter filter}
\label{sec:filter}

Registered; CPU only, no network trained or run. The filter is defined in
\S\ref{sec:methods:est} of the main text: state $\log\Ti{}$, a random walk with process
variance $q$ per 10\,ms; observation variance $r$; a Huber-type update weighted by
$\min(1, k\sqrt{S}/|v|)$; a drive $b_c\,\Delta c_t + b_g\,\Delta g_t$ in the prediction
step, with $g$ the mean logarithm of column 07 of the BES array minus that of column 05
and each change clipped at 0.2. The output for row $t$ uses CES values before $t$ and
BES up to $t$; altering the CES values from row $t$ on leaves it unchanged (a
registered check). Per fold the parameters minimise the mean squared error in eV on the
discharges that are not in the test fold: first $q$, $r$, $k$ without drive (KF0), then
the gains with those held (Kc: $b_c$ only; Kg: $b_g$ only; KFX: both).

declared forking paths: the test error of a filter with an unclipped drive was seen once

abandoned there because it degenerated in three of five folds. r2 is therefore the
development set and is not pooled into the confirmation; every statement is read on
re-partitions r3 to r9. The clip is fixed at 0.2, which every fold of r2 chose; refits
at 0.1 and 0.4 give $+0.048$ $[+0.040, +0.056]$ and $+0.048$ $[+0.039, +0.057]$ for
KFX over KF0.

\section{The floor, the gap to the offline estimate, and persistence}
\label{sec:yardsticks}

Registered; CPU only, the 40 stored twins run forward for their signed estimates. Rows
of the pooled cross-validation, re-partitions r2 to r9; interval: a re-partition drawn,
then its discharges resampled (2,000 draws). \emph{Floor.} On the triple rows, scored
rows $t$ whose frames $t-1$ and $t+1$ are scored, consecutive and 10\,ms apart
(1{,}085{,}452 of the 1{,}536{,}645 scored rows), $s^2$, minus the covariance of successive
increments of the reported value, is 18{,}415 $[15{,}969, 21{,}226]$\,eV$^2$ (root mean
square \FloorRms\,eV). It is a lower bound of the mean frame-to-frame independent
variance if the increments of the underlying value are not negatively correlated.
\emph{MSE over the floor} and \emph{removable-error skill} of $A$ over $R$,
$(\mathrm{MSE}_R - \mathrm{MSE}_A)/(\mathrm{MSE}_R - s^2)$, use the MSE of each estimator
on the same triple rows, where the errors are smaller than on all rows; the root-mean-square
errors of the main text are on all rows, so the ratios do not follow from them. The
reciprocal of the ratio is the share of an estimator's error that the floor accounts
for. \emph{Gap closure} of $A$, on all scored rows: $(\mathrm{MSE}_\mathrm{GP} -
\mathrm{MSE}_A)/(\mathrm{MSE}_\mathrm{GP} - \mathrm{MSE}_\mathrm{offline\,GP})$. The floor
bounds what an estimate from the CES history can remove; an estimate that reads a
present-frame signal correlated with the conditions of that frame's fit is not bounded
by it, and the persistence below speaks to that.
\emph{Persistence}: for a correction $d_t = A_t - B_t$, $\beta_h$ is the regression
coefficient of $y_{t+h} - B_t$ on $d_t$, on rows whose frames $t$ to $t+h$ are
consecutive and scored, and $\rho_h = \beta_h/\beta_0$. A correction that describes the
plasma should keep $\rho_1$ near the reference of a correction that cannot know the fit
of frame $t$ (the twin minus KF0); one that describes the fit of frame $t$ should not.

\paragraph{Statements.} All five hold. On the triple rows every estimator's mean squared
error is above the floor (N1, Table~\ref{tab:floor}). The recurrent estimator closes more than half of the
gap between the causal and the offline GP (N2: \GapNet{}). Its fast-channel correction
lasts into the next frame (N3: $\rho_1$ = \RhoNet{}), as does the filter's (N4:
\RhoKFX{}), and the part that does not last does not shrink further three frames later
(N5: $\rho_3 - \rho_1$ = \RhoDrop{}; Table~\ref{tab:persist}).

correction from the CES history alone, so the part specific to the frame (its fit, or a
fluctuation inside it that the fast sample sees) is about a fifth by the point value

\paragraph{How large could a loss of beam heating be? An estimate.} This is an estimate
from published formulas, not a measurement. With the critical energy, the slowing-down
time on electrons and the thermalisation time of~\citet{Heidbrink1994BehaviourFast}
(their equations 7, 9 and 10) for a 95\,keV deuteron, $Z_\mathrm{eff} = 2$ and
$\ln\Lambda = 17$, at pedestal-top parameters ($T_e = 0.5$\,keV,
$n_e = 2.5\times10^{19}$\,m$^{-3}$) the critical energy is 9.3\,keV, the slowing-down time
on electrons 33\,ms and the thermalisation time 39\,ms, and 0.17 of the beam energy goes
to the ions; at mid-radius and core parameters the thermalisation takes 81 to 105\,ms.
Beam ions keep slowing down, and so keep heating, after the source stops; on DIII-D the
measured slowing-down time agreed with classical theory within about
30\%~\citep{Heidbrink1988ComparisonExperimental}. How much of the heating an
interruption removes is not published: in 2022 the first source of the first beam line
could not be used~\citep{Han2024CriterionLong}, and in one discharge of this set the
published beam power falls at the interruptions by at least 1.1\,MW from about 3.5\,MW
(read from figure~2(b) of~\citep{Heo2025ExperimentalIdentification}), about a third; no
paper names the source switched off in 2022. In a published discharge of an earlier
campaign the source that drops to zero about once a second (the second source of the
first beam line, read from figure~1(a)) carried 1.0 of 2.7\,MW~\citep{Kim2018EnhancedFast},
which agrees with a third. In a
zero-dimensional balance $dW/dt = P_h(t) - W/\tau_E$, with $P_h$ the beam source passed
through that slowing-down, $\tau_E$ of 50 or 85\,ms (a range around the IPB98(y,2)
scaling~\citep{ITER1999Chapter2} at published KSTAR H-mode
parameters~\citep{Kim2020PedestalElectron}, with an
assumed density) and a third of the heating removed for 10.5\,ms, the fall averaged over
the first frame after the return is 1.8 to 2.9\% of the level (5.3 to 8.6\% if all the
heating were removed), the largest fall comes 26 to 31\,ms after the return, and it scales
with the length of the interruption (6.1 to 9.4\% for an interruption of 27\,ms). Ohmic
and other heating are neglected, so these are upper estimates; every number of this
paragraph is reproduced by a script of the public repository. This size is comparable with the fall of the ECEI level above (to 0.938 at its
lowest) and smaller than the first-row fall of \Ti{} (0.186 of the level), and no dose is
seen in the events of two and three absent frames. A published size agrees: at a
blank of about 10\,ms, which serves the charge-exchange and the motional-Stark
measurements, ``the central electron temperature and stored energy decrease by less than
10\%''~\citep{Kwak2019MeasurementFast}, with the whole beam power blanked in its example
(read from figure~3(d), a 2017 discharge); for a third of the power that is a few per
cent. Since the stored energy and the position of the plasma are linked, it also bears on
a change of the equilibrium at the blips. Published for KSTAR blips are a
plasma displacement and ion-temperature fluctuations, in particular in the
core~\citep{Kim2018EnhancedFast} (in its
figure~1(a) the pedestal \Ti{} and rotation traces dip at each drop of the source, read
from the figure). Conversely, a beam switched on perturbs the plasma promptly: beam pulses
of 10 and 20\,ms into an ohmic KSTAR plasma raised the
rotation~\citep{Lee2016ValidationToroidal}, and a switching-on beam perturbs the
electrons~\citep{Zhao2023RefinedInterpretation}.
The KSTAR CES group itself writes that on KSTAR the modulation for the charge-exchange
background uses the main heating beam rather than a dedicated diagnostic beam, ``which
may potentially cause plasma disturbances'', and that frequent modulation ``can disturb
plasma conditions, affecting the accuracy of CES measurements'' (abstract and \S1
of~\citep{Lee2026NeuralNetworkbased}). With a fixed chord in a steep gradient, a displacement of the plasma changes the value
read at the chord, and the files carry no equilibrium to test it. Which part of the fall
is a response to the lost heating, a displacement, or the measurement of the first frame
is not decided here.

\paragraph{The wall magnetic field at the interruptions (exploratory).} Registered as
statements, read here as an exploratory note because two of its four statements were
written as if the relative polarity of the coils were known, which it is not. On the raw
2\,MHz signals of the two Mirnov coils at different toroidal positions in the twelve
discharges with raw streams, the signal integrated from the moment the beam goes off
(36 interruptions of one absent frame; a drift of the integral was removed with half of
the pseudo-events of each discharge and the other half kept as control) changes while the
beam is off, holds for about 50\,ms after its return and has fallen by 60\,ms, in every
discharge; the two coils change with opposite signs, as they do at candidate edge bursts
and at pseudo-events, so their effective polarities are opposite and the wall field
changes alike at both positions. This
indicates a change of the equilibrium at the interruptions; its size cannot be given (the
coils are not calibrated in the files), two positions cannot exclude a non-axisymmetric
structure, and what part of the fall of \Ti{} at the chord it accounts for is not known.
It is not used as a cause in the main text.

\section{MAST data}
\label{sec:data:mast}
The second device enters through the public FAIR-MAST
archive~\citep{Jackson2024FairMast}, whose MAST data are released under the licence
CC~BY-SA~4.0; we read its level-1 source of the time-resolved charge-exchange
system~\citep{Conway2006HighThroughput}: \Ti{}, toroidal
velocity \Vi{} and their reported fit errors on many chords at 5\,ms frames, campaigns
M6--M9 (shots 14708--30473; 2006--2013). A sample is a \emph{good fit} if \Ti{} is
finite and positive, below 5\,keV, with a positive reported error and a relative error
below 0.5 (the \emph{loose} rule) or below 0.1 (the \emph{strict} rule). The beam
block of a shot is the span from the first to the last frame with at least ten good
chords; a chord is scored if it is good in at least 90\% of the block. Each chord is
assigned a normalized radius $\rho = (R - R_\mathrm{axis})/(R_\mathrm{out} -
R_\mathrm{axis})$ from the magnetic-axis and outer-boundary radii of the catalogue
(one pair per discharge; low-field side only).

The MAST data were used in two stages. A list of 300 shots, drawn from the catalogue
alone, served to develop the analysis (\S\ref{app:mast}); a breakdown of that
set by radius, made after its pooled result was known, suggested that the measurement
weakens and the series becomes noise-dominated towards the edge. That suggestion was
then written down as a hypothesis with its decision rules and tested once on the
shots that had not been read: 3{,}686 discharges with 5\,ms frames and catalogue
geometry (of 4{,}030 files with the catalogue quality flag ``Not Checked''; M6 623, M7
1{,}520, M8 938, M9 605), of which 3{,}409 have a beam block under the loose rule. No
hyperparameter was retuned on them. Section~\ref{sec:regime} reports this
confirmation set.

\section{The increments on the chord are close to white noise; on MAST the same statistic falls towards the edge}
\label{sec:regime}

Let $y_t$ be the CES series on a chord and $d_t = y_t - y_{t-1}$ its 10\,ms increment.
If the plasma value is constant over a few samples and each sample carries independent
fit noise, successive increments share one noise term with opposite sign and their
correlation is $-0.5$. If the plasma evolves smoothly and the noise is small,
successive increments have the same sign and the correlation is positive. The lag-1
autocorrelation of increments is therefore a \emph{regime number}: it says whether the
last CES sample differs from the next one mostly because of the fit or mostly because
of the plasma, and with it which simple estimator is the right yardstick.

\paragraph{KSTAR.} On the KSTAR chord the regime number is close to the white-noise
value. Its median over discharges is $-0.416$ $[-0.429, -0.402]$ for \Ti{} (601
discharges; interquartile range $-0.491$ to $-0.294$) and $-0.349$
$[-0.384, -0.322]$ for \Vrot{} (382 discharges). This is a descriptive statistic, not a registered
test. It agrees with two independent readings: on the rows between two scored frames
the mean-square floor of \S\ref{sec:yardsticks} is two thirds to three quarters of the
mean squared error of the causal estimators (half for hold); and the ratio of the 10\,ms scatter of \Ti{} divided by
\Ti{} to the 10\,ms scatter of \Vrot{} divided by the thermal Doppler width of the
carbon line at that \Ti{} (the Gaussian standard deviation in velocity,
$\sqrt{\Ti{}/m_\mathrm{C}}$) is close to $\sqrt2$, and mostly above it (1.395--1.532 in
eight split--population cells), $\sqrt2$ being the value for independent Gaussian line-fit errors on width and shift. In this regime averaging removes noise:
a running mean has \Ti{} skill $+0.26$ to $+0.34$ over hold on the four splits
and the causal GP $+0.31$ to $+0.35$. PCHIP passes exactly through noisy anchors, so
any smoother of the CES past gains on it, and the deciding CES-only comparison for a
learned estimator is the causal GP.

\paragraph{MAST: a registered test of the dependence on radius.} One chord cannot
show that the noise-limited regime belongs to a weak edge measurement. MAST has chords
from the core to the edge, and the hypothesis was tested on the 3{,}686 discharges of
the confirmation set (\S\ref{sec:data:mast}); the visible series there is every
second 5\,ms frame, so its increments are 10\,ms apart as on KSTAR.
Table~\ref{tab:edge} and Fig.~\ref{fig:edge} give the result. Two statements were
registered as primary. (i) \emph{The measurement weakens towards the edge.} The share
of failed fits is 0.270 $[0.262, 0.277]$ at $\rho = 0.5$--0.7 and exceeds it by
$+0.275$ $[+0.268, +0.282]$ at 0.8--0.9, by $+0.392$ $[+0.382, +0.401]$ at 0.9--0.95
and by $+0.463$ $[+0.451, +0.474]$ at 0.95--1.0; the median relative \Ti{} error rises
from 0.044 to 0.108, 0.186 and 0.347. Inside $\rho = 0.5$ the failure share is flat
(0.215 and 0.214), and the relative error is flat inside 0.7. (ii)
\emph{The regime number falls towards the edge.} Under the loose rule, which keeps
the weak samples, the \Ti{} number is $+0.299$ $[+0.285, +0.311]$ at 0.5--0.7 and
lower by $-0.296$ $[-0.320, -0.271]$ at 0.8--0.9 and by $-0.347$ $[-0.380, -0.312]$ at
0.9--1.0, where it is $-0.049$ $[-0.078, -0.016]$, an interval below zero; taken
separately, the two halves of that bin have intervals that include zero
(Table~\ref{tab:edge}), so the sign at the very edge rests on the merged bin, which
was the registered one. Both statements hold. Rotation behaves the same way: the \Vi{} number falls from $+0.330$
at 0.5--0.7 by $-0.309$ $[-0.335, -0.289]$ and $-0.402$ $[-0.434, -0.371]$, to
$+0.021$ $[-0.004, +0.037]$ and $-0.072$ $[-0.107, -0.044]$.

Three qualifications bound this result. First, the fit rule decides the sign at the
edge: under the strict rule the edge chords that pass are still trend-dominated
($+0.234$ $[+0.172, +0.339]$ at 0.9--1.0), although the number is lower than at
mid-radius there too ($-0.209$ $[-0.272, -0.153]$ at 0.8--0.9 and $-0.108$
$[-0.176, -0.003]$ at 0.9--1.0). The fall towards a noise-dominated series is a
statement about an edge measurement that includes its weak samples. Second, it does
not hold in every campaign: at 0.9--1.0 the loose-rule \Ti{} number is $-0.207$,
$-0.176$ and $-0.104$ in M6, M7 and M8, every interval below zero, but $+0.200$
$[+0.163, +0.294]$ in M9, where the difference to mid-radius, $-0.060$
$[-0.106, +0.035]$, includes zero. Third, the MAST edge does not reach the KSTAR
value of $-0.416$: at either candidate radius of the KSTAR chord MAST is no longer
trend-dominated, but a difference between the devices remains that radius does not
account for. The two devices differ in exposure, chord geometry and beam-block length,
and the remainder is not attributed to a single cause.

Two secondary statements concern the yardstick. A running mean with a 15\,ms memory,
which costs $-0.968$ $[-1.021, -0.918]$ in skill against hold in the core
($\rho < 0.7$, loose rule), costs $-0.124$ $[-0.285, +0.001]$ at 0.9--1.0, an
interval that includes zero (difference $+0.845$ $[+0.682, +0.975]$): the penalty of
averaging disappears at the edge, but averaging does not become a gain as it is on
KSTAR, and under the strict rule the penalty remains ($-0.741$ $[-1.776, -0.175]$).
The statement that the skill of a causal GP over hold shrinks at the edge did
not hold: the difference to the core is $-0.025$ $[-0.182, +0.064]$ and $-0.020$
$[-0.163, +0.051]$, both including zero.

\paragraph{The same number orders the estimators inside KSTAR.} If the regime number
decides the yardstick, it should do so from discharge to discharge on one device.
This was registered as a prediction before the per-discharge values were computed.
Across 280 KSTAR discharges, the rank correlation between the regime number of a
discharge and the difference between causal-GP skill and running-mean skill is
$+0.338$ $[+0.213, +0.451]$ for \Ti{} (cut; $+0.375$ $[+0.262, +0.477]$ inclusive)
and $+0.574$ $[+0.442, +0.680]$ for \Vrot{} (170 discharges). Averaging gains $+0.393$
$[+0.375, +0.412]$ over hold in the most noise-dominated third of the
discharges and $+0.087$ $[-0.012, +0.166]$ in the least noise-dominated third. A
second prediction did not hold: the margin of the recurrent estimator over the causal
GP was expected to grow with the regime number, and its rank correlation is $-0.081$
$[-0.207, +0.048]$; the margin excludes zero in all three thirds
(\S\ref{app:more}). The regime number and the errors are computed from the same
rows, and only 3.2\% of the \Ti{} discharges have a positive regime number, so the
relation is established inside the noise-dominated range.

\paragraph{What the CES past leaves.} MAST stores a fit error per sample, so the
residual of a CES-only estimator can be compared with the fit noise. In the
development set, the best estimator that does not use the following sample leaves a
mean squared residual of 2.57 $[1.94, 3.40]$ (\Ti{}) times what the reported errors
predict, most of it in a tail, and for \Ti{} in two of four campaigns
(\S\ref{app:mast}). The KSTAR files carry no per-sample fit error, so the same
quantity cannot be formed there. What can be said for KSTAR is that three CES-only
estimators built in different ways lie within 3\% of each other in \Ti{} RMSE (164.3
to 169.2\,eV, Table~\ref{tab:ladder}); whether what remains above them is fit noise or plasma
signal is a question the fast diagnostics bear on (\S\ref{sec:fast}) and do not
settle.

\section{The same question on a second device: the MAST pedestal top}
\label{sec:mastped}
MAST has no diagnostic faster than its charge-exchange system at the chord, but it
has an independent measurement at the same radius: Thomson scattering gives $T_e$ and
$n_e$ along the mid-plane once or twice per 5\,ms frame. The question of this paper
can therefore be put on a second device in a weaker form: when a frame is hidden,
does a measurement at the location of the chord lower the error of a causal estimate
at the pedestal top? A chord was placed by its distance to the edge of the Thomson

odd-numbered discharges of the set of \S\ref{sec:regime}. Six statements were then
registered, and the models were trained once on those discharges and applied once to
the 1{,}651 even-numbered ones (Table~\ref{tab:mastped}).

\section{CES-only estimators on the MAST development set}
\label{app:mast}

The development set is 300 shots (75 per campaign, catalogue quality ``Not
Checked''), of which 264 carry the arrays; they alternate within each campaign between a tuning
set (133 shots, 132 with 5\,ms frames) and a test set (131 shots, 129 with 5\,ms frames). Every second
frame of the beam block is hidden and estimated from the visible frames of the same
chord. Under the strict rule the test set holds 58{,}844 scored rows from 88 shots.
Besides the estimators of \S\ref{sec:methods:est} (the causal GP here uses 8 past
samples with tuned hyperparameters), a slope-state filter (a Kalman filter with level
and slope states) is included. The reduced chi-square \chir{} is the squared residual
divided by the variance that the reported errors alone predict for that estimator; a
residual that is fit noise only gives a mean of 1 and a median of 0.455.

\paragraph{Independence of a hidden frame.} For spacings $k = 1, 2, 4$ frames, the
robust variance $S_k$ of the second difference $y_{t+k} - 2y_t + y_{t-k}$ equals the
noise variance plus a signal term that grows with $k$; if the noise is white in time,
$T_a = [S_1 - S_2 + (S_4 - S_2)/2]/S_2 \geq 0$, whereas a positive noise correlation
between adjacent frames lowers it. The rule fixed in advance rejects independence if
the interval of the median $T_a$ lies below zero. It is $+0.453$ $[+0.341, +0.565]$
for \Ti{} and $+0.642$ $[+0.208, +1.148]$ for \Vi{}. Compared with the variance the
reported errors predict, the same second differences give a ratio to the Gaussian
value of 0.540 $[0.463, 0.637]$ for \Ti{} and 2.314 $[1.943, 2.759]$ for \Vi{}: the
reported \Vi{} errors are smaller than the frame-to-frame scatter.

\section{Lagged association with beam power and D-alpha on MAST}
\label{app:coupling}

The KSTAR files carry no beam-power or D-alpha signal. On MAST both are public, so
the association of 5\,ms increments of \Ti{} and rotation with increments of the
injected beam power and of mid-plane D-alpha was computed over lags of $-100$ to
$+100$\,ms. The statements, their directions and the wording to use if they failed
were registered before any such relation was computed. The lag was chosen on 1{,}582
discharges and every number below is from the other 1{,}591; intervals are from a
bootstrap over discharges.

\sloppy Rotation increments are correlated with beam-power increments 45\,ms earlier
($+0.112$ $[+0.107, +0.117]$) and \Ti{} increments with those 50\,ms earlier ($+0.082$

\section{Which rows carry the gain: edge bursts in the raw beam-emission stream}
\label{sec:bursts}
For twelve discharges of the data set the 500\,kHz BES stream was delivered. It
allows two things. The state of the beam can be read directly: the
level falls below half its median only during blips of about 10\,ms, 1.5\,s apart in
the two discharges of Fig.~\ref{fig:bursts} and each an absent row of the 10\,ms
file, and the beam is on otherwise. And short bursts of beam emission, which the
10\,ms snapshots do not resolve, can be detected (a 0.2\,ms mean above its 50\,ms running
median by six robust standard deviations). The detector and the row labels were fixed
on one discharge, 31921, and the statements below were registered before any other
discharge was labelled or any model error was broken down. A row is
\emph{burst-active} when at least three bursts fall in the preceding 200\,ms and
\emph{quiet} when none does; rows within 30\,ms after a blip are left out.

Figure~\ref{fig:bursts} shows the two discharges with a published edge
analysis~\citep{Heo2025ExperimentalIdentification}, which places 31921 in the FIRE mode of KSTAR
at 5.40\,s and in H-mode at 8.05\,s. In 31921 bursts occur 0 to 0.8 times per second
between 5.1 and 7.3\,s and 30 to 35 times per second after 7.3\,s, and \Ti{} has no
missing row before 7.4\,s. In both discharges the late phase begins with a rise of
the BES level and of \Ti{} from a few hundred eV to about 2\,keV. The
excursions of more than 1\,keV between adjacent rows in Fig.~\ref{fig:trace} of the main text(a, b) are
in this phase, among the bursts, and are not isolated outliers.

On the eleven other discharges, \Ti{} is missing more often in rows with a burst in
the preceding 10\,ms than in quiet rows in five of the six discharges that have
enough rows of both kinds (0.24 to 0.37 against 0.00 to 0.01 in three of them), and
the median step of \Ti{} between adjacent measured rows is larger in burst-active
rows in seven of seven (231.5 against 32.7\,eV and 322.7 against 28.4\,eV in the two
with the largest contrast). Both registered statements hold. The third, that the hold
error is larger in burst-active rows on at least 80\% of the eligible test
discharges, does not: it is larger on three of four (811.3 against 46.0\,eV on
31923; 132.2 against 146.3\,eV on the one against). Pooled over the six test
discharges with a raw stream, burst-active rows are 0.115 $[0.039, 0.198]$ of the
scored rows and carry 0.604 $[0.056, 0.786]$ of the squared-error reduction of the
network against its history-only twin; the skill of the network over the twin is
$+0.197$ $[+0.109, +0.249]$ in burst-active rows and $+0.135$ $[+0.109, +0.153]$ in
quiet rows. The point estimates place a disproportionate part of the fast-channel gain in
phases with edge bursts, but with six discharges the interval of that part includes
the share of rows, so a concentration is not established; the share is present
outside those phases as well.

Four limits apply. The bursts are defined on beam emission and are consistent with
edge-localized modes; without D-alpha they are not shown to be. The detector is a
threshold on the mean of nine channels: the published analysis of 31921 reports
intermittent bursts at 6.65--7.00\,s~\citep{Heo2025ExperimentalIdentification},
where the detector finds none, so a quiet row is quiet at this threshold only. The
passive edge light that a CES fit has to separate from the charge-exchange line depends on
the edge neutral density and the pedestal impurity
density~\citep{Viezzer2011PassiveEdge}, both of which a burst can change, and the
published analysis uses
the CES ion temperature of these discharges only between beam blips, which are
1.5\,s apart here; how the 10\,ms values between blips are obtained is not recorded
in the files. If the background is interpolated between beam-off frames this far
apart~\citep{Lee2020TwoGaussian}, it is interpolated through the burst phases, and a
frame's background need not be that of the frame. Whether a value in a frame that contains a burst is the temperature is
therefore not decided here; what the paper estimates is the reported value
(\S\ref{sec:data:kstar}). The blips give one check of what the values between them
follow. Over the 1{,}394 blips of 508 discharges that have six contiguous rows before them (one,
two or three absent frames; a wider set than the 1{,}262 events of one absent frame read in
\S\ref{sec:rows} and in the main text), the
CES \Ti{} of the first row after a blip is 0.819 $[0.796, 0.838]$ of its level before
and returns over about 90\,ms. The ECEI level, which does not use the beam, falls to
0.926 $[0.910, 0.939]$ and returns over the same time: the mean deficit of \Ti{} over
the six rows after a blip is 2.33 $[1.99, 2.75]$ times that of the ECEI level, and
blips with a deeper ECEI dip have a deeper \Ti{} dip (rank correlation $+0.251$
$[+0.199, +0.304]$). The beam emission is back to 0.954 of its level by the fourth
row, where \Ti{} is still at 0.877. The time course of the CES value after a blip is
thus that of a diagnostic that uses neither the beam nor the spectrometer, not that of
the beam emission; whether the fall is a change of the plasma is not decided
(\S\ref{sec:rows}, last paragraph). A value is reported in frames without a beam as well: in
one discharge two beam interruptions of 23 and 26\,ms removed no frame, and the rows
exposed during them carry values (979\,eV; 1{,}711 and 1{,}133\,eV, against medians
of 1{,}063 and 1{,}195\,eV before), while elsewhere a beam-off time of about 27\,ms
leaves two or three frames absent (\S\ref{sec:rows}); the files do not say whether
another source or beam remained on during the two longer interruptions. Six discharges carry the model statements, and
the interval of the gain share is wide. By the rule registered with these statements, one of the
three measurement statements having failed, the mechanism is still described as not
identified.

\section{What a large departure of the beam emission is: the heating beam and edge bursts}
\label{sec:twosources}
The response of the estimator to a fast channel is linear in the departure of the
channel from its 100\,ms mean up to about two standard deviations and weaker beyond
three (\S\ref{sec:fast}). Two things move the beam emission that far within one row,
and the ion temperature is expected to follow neither in proportion. \emph{The
heating beam.} BES is line emission of beam atoms excited in the plasma, so its level
follows the beam without a delay that a 10\,ms frame resolves. The temperature
follows an energy balance: beam ions already in the plasma keep heating it while they
slow down~\citep{Stix1972HeatingToroidal}, heat leaves over the confinement time, and
electrons and ions exchange energy over $m_i/2m_e$ electron collision
times~\citep{Wesson2011Tokamaks}. For $T_e = 0.5$\,keV and
$n_e = 2.5\times10^{19}$\,m$^{-3}$, assumed pedestal-top values (\S\ref{sec:rows}), the
slowing-down time of a beam deuteron on electrons is about 33\,ms; it scales as
$T_e^{3/2}/n_e$, so at the line-averaged densities published for discharges of the set
(1.0 to 1.4$\times10^{19}$\,m$^{-3}$, Table~\ref{tab:plasma}) it would be two to two and a
half times as long and a blip would remove an even smaller part of the heating. By the
formulas of~\citet{Heidbrink1994BehaviourFast} evaluated at mid-radius and core
parameters (\S\ref{sec:rows}), beam ions deposited deeper slow down over 50 to 100\,ms or
more. A blip
of about 10\,ms therefore removes the beam light and only part of the heating.
\emph{An edge burst} raises the light at the edge for less than a millisecond. A row
holds one raw sample, so a sample that falls on a burst departs upward by several
standard deviations. A fall of \Ti{} after a burst, which would make a proportional
reading wrong in sign, was registered as a statement and does not hold: over 108
isolated burst onsets of eleven discharges the median ratio of \Ti{} in the 30\,ms
after an onset to its level before is 1.007 $[0.947, 1.056]$ (bursts inside trains
are not covered by that measure).

Both are found in the files. In the first row after a blip 0.357 $[0.319, 0.396]$ of
the BES channel values lie beyond three standard deviations of their 100\,ms mean,
against 0.004 $[0.003, 0.004]$ in all other rows: 0.353 $[0.319, 0.388]$ of all such
values sit in rows that make up 0.0058 of the values. Outside those rows, in the twelve
discharges with a raw stream, 0.564 of the 110 channel values above $+3$ standard
deviations have a detected burst within 1\,ms of the time stamp (0.015 of all rows;
ratio 37.2 $[25.0, 70.5]$), and 0.727 of the 110 values below $-3$ fall on a short
dip of the raw beam-emission level (0.016 of all rows; ratio 46.4 $[25.0, 121.9]$).
These two counts were made per channel value after the fact; the registered count,
per row, found ten rows above $+3$, eight of them at a burst, fewer than the twenty
that the registered test required.

What \Ti{} does with such a departure was read on the first row after a blip
(1{,}203 blips of 449 discharges), in three groups of the mean departure $d$ of the
nine BES channels, with two registered statements. The statement that the ratio of
\Ti{} to its level before the blip does not depend on $d$ does not hold: the ratio is
0.886 $[0.844, 0.917]$ for $d > -2$, 0.786 $[0.730, 0.820]$ for $-3 < d \leq -2$ and
0.784 $[0.755, 0.811]$ for $d \leq -3$ (last minus first, $-0.103$
$[-0.147, -0.053]$). The statement that for $d \leq -3$ the change is smaller in size
than a proportional reading at 50\,eV per standard deviation, the summed BES gain of
the filter of \S\ref{sec:character}, holds: $-164.5$\,eV against $-219.3$\,eV, a
difference of $+54.8$ $[+28.7, +81.2]$\,eV. Read after the fact, the two groups below
$-2$ do not differ ($-0.002$ $[-0.052, +0.057]$) although their mean departures differ
by 1.9 standard deviations. In rows that are not after a blip the mean change of
\Ti{} from its last value is within 5\,eV of the proportional reading for departures
within one standard deviation and far from it beyond three: $-54.0$
$[-113.5, +2.7]$\,eV at a mean departure of $-4.59$ and $+65.3$
$[-17.5, +154.1]$\,eV at $+4.18$, where proportionality gives $-229.5$ and
$+209.0$\,eV. The measured relation thus has the form the estimator learned:
proportional for small departures, and not growing in proportion beyond two to three
standard deviations.

Beyond three the estimator is not switched off. In the first row after a blip with
$d \leq -3$ the causal variant predicts a change of $-147.1$ to $-179.5$\,eV on the
four splits, where $-179.9$ to $-243.2$\,eV was measured and the history-only twin
predicts $-12.3$ to $+4.8$\,eV; its skill over the twin in these rows is $+0.494$ to
$+0.677$ (four of four intervals exclude zero; read after the fact on the rows of
\S\ref{sec:thin}). The gate of the filter of \S\ref{sec:character} removes the term
of a channel beyond its threshold; that is a property of the filter, not of the
network.

MAST allows the first reading to be checked on a step of the heating that leaves the
measurement beam untouched: it has two beams, the charge-exchange system views one,
and both powers are public. Two statements on the response of \Ti{} to a switch of
the other beam, its direction and its delay, were registered and hold, but the
registered definition counted one switch at several adjacent frames and had no
control for the evolution of the discharge. The numbers quoted are therefore a
reading after the fact, with one event per switch and with windows without a switch
at the same time of the discharge as control. After a switch-on (542 events) \Ti{}
is above control by $+0.068$ $[+0.044, +0.104]$ of its level in the first 5\,ms frame
after the switch and by $+0.278$ $[+0.181, +0.356]$ at 50 to 80\,ms; half of the
latter is reached after 15\,ms. At normalized radii of 0.7 and above (93 events) the
two values are $+0.170$ $[+0.093, +0.213]$ and $+0.560$ $[+0.366, +0.723]$. After a
switch-off (78 events) they are $-0.034$ $[-0.067, +0.002]$ and $-0.075$
$[-0.165, -0.018]$, and a delay is not established. On that device the ion
temperature answers a step of the heating over tens of milliseconds; this is not a
measurement of the KSTAR response.

By the rule registered with these statements, the first having failed, the weaker
response of the estimator beyond three standard deviations is not attributed to the
beam: \Ti{} after a blip does fall with the departure of the beam emission, up to
between two and three standard deviations. What the checks establish is narrower.
Departures beyond three standard deviations are mostly the return of the beam after
a blip, or samples on bursts and dips of the raw stream; the measured change of \Ti{}
does not grow in proportion to them; and whether the value in the first row after a
blip is the temperature or the response of the CES analysis to a returning beam is
not decided by the files.

\section{The median row, a 20-parameter reading, and a second stage}
\label{sec:character}

A mean-squared-error skill is decided by few rows. On KSTAR the 1\% of rows with the
largest squared \Ti{} error of the recurrent estimator carry 48\% (cut) and 83\%
(inclusive) of its total. On a typical row the estimator carries its last value: its
median absolute \Ti{} error, 47.2\,eV, is close to that of hold (46.1\,eV) and
above the typical frame-to-frame scatter of a row (39.1 to 42.8\,eV, \S\ref{sec:target}), while PCHIP reaches 39.4\,eV by
averaging with the following sample (median-absolute-error skill $-0.198$
$[-0.259, -0.135]$). For rotation the median row is worse than hold (4.3
against 3.7\,km/s). The aggregate skill is thus a deficit on the bulk offset by a
gain on the tail. Under a symmetric 1\% trim and under a Huber loss at the noise
bound the \Ti{} skill over PCHIP stays $+0.210$ and $+0.156$ (cut, both intervals
excluding zero). The median is a property of the estimator as much as of the regime: a network
trained on squared error moves every row a little in order to avoid large errors. A
separately registered scalar filter with heavy-tailed (Student-$t$) measurement
noise, run causally on the CES history, has a median absolute \Ti{} error 0.906 to
0.920 of that of hold in all eight split--population cells, and it is not the
mean-squared-error estimator. The estimator with the lowest mean error and the one
with the lowest median error are therefore different, and an application has to say
which it needs. Which rows carry the mean gain, and whether they are changes of the
plasma or fit excursions, cannot be decided without per-exposure fit errors.

\paragraph{A 20-parameter reading of the ion-temperature branch.} (Superseded by the
five-parameter filter of \S\ref{sec:filter}, whose form was fixed before its confirmation
set was read; kept as the earlier reading.) The weights of the
trained networks carry no stable reading, but the input--output
response~\citep{Maheswaranathan2020HowRecurrent} does: the \Ti{} output is an
exponentially weighted average of past observed \Ti{} plus the high-pass fast-channel
response described in \S\ref{sec:fast}. Written as a filter and trained from scratch
under the identical protocol,
\begin{equation}
  \hat y_t = m_t + \sum_{c=1}^{15} a_c\, d_{c,t}\,
     \sigma\!\Bigl(\frac{\theta-|d_{c,t}|}{0.5}\Bigr) + b_0,
  \qquad
  m_t = m_{t-1} + K(s)\,(y^\mathrm{obs} - m_{t-1}),
  \label{eq:surrogate}
\end{equation}
with $d_{c,t}$ the fast channel minus its mean over the preceding ten steps, $m_t$
updated only when a new \Ti{} measurement arrives, $s$ the time since the previous
one, $K(s) = K_\infty + (K_0 - K_\infty)e^{-s/s_K}$ and $\sigma$ the logistic
function. On the four cut splits the learned values are $K_0 = 0.27$--$0.30$,
$K_\infty = 0.48$--$0.53$, $s_K = 39$--$47$\,ms, $\theta = 2.9$--$3.0$ standard
deviations, and summed gains of $+48$ to $+53$\,eV per standard deviation for BES and
$+44$ to $+50$ for ECEI. This filter keeps 72--78\% of the recurrent estimator's skill
over PCHIP on the four cut splits and 0.31 to 0.50 of its skill over the causal GP.
Three qualifications bound what it shows. The form of the fast-channel term was read
from the network's Jacobian on the test rows on which the filter is scored, so the
retention is post hoc. It holds in the cut population only. And as a standalone
estimator the filter has skill $+0.02$ to $+0.07$ over the causal GP with the
interval excluding zero on 2 of 4 cut splits; it is an interpretation of the network,
not a replacement for it. The rising gain $K(s)$ is a property of the joint fit: when
the filter is refit with every fast-channel gain set to zero, the optimal gain is
flat or falls with the gap (0.30--0.32 at 10\,ms, 0.23--0.26 at 50\,ms).

\paragraph{A second stage: which rows will be wrong, and a correction.} Every
estimator above gives one value. Whether the size and the sign of its error can be
known at the row was tested on the frozen causal variant, without retraining it.
Fifteen quantities known at the row (nine summaries of the 100\,ms fast-channel
window, the age of the last CES sample, the hold value, and the differences of the
network from hold and from the causal GP with their absolute values) feed
gradient-boosted regressions. These were fitted on the validation discharges of each
split (127 or 128) and applied once to its test rows, with four statements registered
beforehand; all four hold in the cut population. (i) The tenth of rows with the
largest predicted error carries 0.472 to 0.545 of the squared error (four of four
splits above the registered 0.30). (ii) A 90\% interval whose half-width follows the
predicted size covers 0.867 to 0.888 of the rows, with a median half-width 0.499 to
0.752 of that of a constant interval (three of four). (iii) A switch that uses hold
where the network is predicted to be the worse of the two has a median absolute error
0.827 to 0.940 of that of the network and 0.881 to 0.937 of that of hold, at a
mean-squared-error skill of $+0.000$ to $+0.011$ over the network (four of four).
(iv) A correction of the signed residual has skill $+0.053$ to $+0.082$ over the
causal variant (four of four intervals exclude zero) and a median absolute error
0.831 to 0.936 of it; in a statement registered afterwards it has $+0.020$ to
$+0.053$ over a linear combination of the network, the causal GP and hold fitted on
the same rows (three of four), so it is a correction and not only an average of
estimators. The same models fitted on half of the test discharges, about 48, had
missed their registered bars (the correction $+0.005$ to $+0.023$, every interval
including zero): what limits the second stage is the number of discharges it is
fitted on. With values above 3\,keV kept, the correction does not lower the mean
squared error by an amount that can be told from zero ($-0.008$ to $+0.041$, one of
four), while its median error is 0.644 to 0.904 of that of the network. One step of
the correction takes 1.257\,ms at the median. The causal variant is therefore not the
best estimator that its inputs allow, and the statement above that a typical row is
not improved holds for a single stage only. In a further registered batch the same
models were fitted on cross-fitted predictions of the training discharges together
with the validation rows (the causal variant retrained on three of four folds of the
training discharges and run on the fourth; 16 runs): the correction then has skill
$+0.078$ to $+0.113$ over the causal variant and $+0.022$ to $+0.035$ over the
correction fitted on validation discharges alone (three of four intervals exclude
zero), the interval covers 0.891 to 0.905 of the rows, and the median absolute error
of the corrected estimate is 0.861 to 0.924 of that of hold, with the interval below
1 on four of four splits (40.3, 45.8, 42.8 and 38.4\,eV against 44.2, 49.5, 49.8 and
43.2\,eV for hold on the rows scored by that batch, which differ from those of
Table~\ref{tab:primary}). Two limits apply: the second stage is a boosted tree fitted after the
network, not trained with it, and it holds for the mean with values above 3\,keV
excluded only. The same model, fitted on out-of-fold estimates of the training
discharges under the pooled protocol, is the second stage of the estimator of the main
text (\S\ref{sec:twostage}). Whether a CES value will be unusable
(missing or above 3\,keV) is itself predictable from the fast-channel window: the area
under the ROC curve is 0.961 to 0.974, against 0.903 to 0.949 from the history of
unusable rows alone (difference $+0.025$ to $+0.059$, four of four splits).

\section{Rotation}
\label{sec:vrot}

Corrected for target noise, the correlation of fast-channel increments with \Vrot{}
increments has a 95\% bound $|r| \leq 0.044$ over every lag from 0 to 200\,ms and
every window from 10 to 200\,ms, against the \Ti{} lower bound of $+0.127$. This
bounds a linear association and does not prove its absence. It is the measured
counterpart of the asymmetry built into the two branches. The
Mirnov signal at 100\,Hz equals a single raw sample of the 2\,MHz stream at the grid
time on 24 of 24 coils checked, so mode rotation in the kHz band is aliased away, and
no torque or beam-power channel exists. On MAST, where beam power is public, rotation
increments are associated with beam-power increments 45\,ms earlier ($+0.112$
$[+0.107, +0.117]$), but that association comes from beam switching and is not
one-sided in time; away from beam steps it is $+0.016$ $[+0.010, +0.022]$
(\S\ref{app:coupling}). The statement is scoped to standard CES at
10\,ms on this chord; edge rotation inside an ELM
cycle~\citep{Viezzer2020Dynamics} is a different measurement.

Rotation is therefore estimated by the branch that reads the CES past only
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}); whether fast channels fed to that branch would lower its
error was not tested.
Among estimators that do not use the next CES sample this branch has the lowest mean
squared error on the first split (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). Its skill over hold is $+0.265$
$[+0.198, +0.326]$ (cut), with the interval excluding zero on all four splits in both
populations, and the same holds over a validation-tuned running mean of the rotation
past, so the estimate is not reducible to a running mean. Over the causal GP the
pooled interval excludes zero in both populations, but per split it excludes zero on
1 of 4 cut splits and 2 of 4 inclusive ones, so that margin is established in the
pooled readout only. Against PCHIP, which uses the following sample, the interval
excludes zero in the cut population by a narrow margin and includes zero in the
inclusive one, so a margin over PCHIP is not established; against the offline GP the
offline smoother has the lower error in the inclusive population. In the thinned
replay of \S\ref{sec:thin}, whose rows are not under the 1{,}000\,km/s rule and are
therefore a different population from Table~\ref{tab:headline}, the skill of this
branch over hold is $+0.361$ to $+0.417$ at 10\,ms and $+0.282$ to $+0.352$ at
100\,ms, on four of four splits.

\section{The rotation scoring rule}
\label{app:vrotrule}

As first registered, without any rule for rotation fit failures, the pooled skill of
the rotation branch over PCHIP was $+0.313$ $[+0.072, +0.701]$. That interval rested on
36--41 of about 71{,}700 rows per repeat (0.05\%) whose target, hold anchor or
PCHIP anchor exceeds $|\Vrot| = 1{,}000$\,km/s; those rows carry 63--78\% of PCHIP's
squared error. The rule that removes them from every estimator was set after that
first analysis and is applied at scoring only: the history inputs still contain those
values and no model was retrained. Under the rule the pooled skill over PCHIP is
$+0.092$ $[+0.0003, +0.170]$ (cut) and $+0.090$ $[-0.010, +0.177]$ (inclusive), and it
includes zero in both populations at thresholds of 500 and 2{,}000\,km/s. All rotation
numbers in this paper are under the rule.

\paragraph{How much of the margin is due to the fast channels.} The comparison above
is between a full-input estimator and CES-only estimators, so the margin could come
from the fast channels or from a better use of the CES past. The direct measurement
is the same network retrained with a \Ti{} branch that reads the CES history only,
scored on the same rows (Table~\ref{tab:honly}). The rule fixed before the first runs
was that the fast-channel increment is claimed if the interval of the full network's
skill over the history-only one lies above zero on at least three of the four cut
splits. In those first runs it did on four of four, but the history-only network had
stopped at its 30-epoch cap on three of the four splits and might have been
undertrained, which would overstate the increment. A second set of runs was therefore
registered with the same rule: both networks were retrained under an 80-epoch cap.
The history-only network then stopped after 49, 46, 11 and 58 epochs and the full
network after 25, 20, 20 and 42, and the increment is $+0.067$, $+0.064$, $+0.117$
and $+0.069$, every interval excluding zero. Training the history-only network 16 to
28 epochs longer changed its own test error by $-0.006$ to $+0.014$; the cap was not
what produced the increment (the first runs are in \S\ref{app:more}). With
values above 3\,keV excluded, then, the full network has a lower \Ti{} error than the same
network without the fast channels by $+0.064$ to $+0.117$.

The two populations differ in where the margin over the causal GP comes from. With
values above 3\,keV excluded, the history-only network is separated from the causal GP on two
of four splits ($+0.011$ to $+0.074$), and the margin of the full network over the
causal GP ($+0.078$ to $+0.138$ on the same splits) is largely the fast-channel
increment. With values above 3\,keV kept, the increment is $+0.016$ to $+0.024$ and its
interval excludes zero on two of four splits, while the history-only network alone
has $+0.102$ to $+0.155$ over the causal GP on four of four: there the margin comes
mainly from how the recurrent state handles a CES history that contains failed fits.
The fast-channel statement is therefore a statement about the cut population.

\paragraph{A causal variant.} The network above standardizes the fast channels with
the mean and variance of the whole discharge. Variants that do not were registered
and scored on the same test rows, with this rule: the word causal is used for a
variant whose \Ti{} skill over the causal GP has an interval above zero on at least
three of four cut splits, both on the file-level splits and on a split by time (train
on the 416 earliest discharge files, 413 of which have usable rows, test on the 97
latest; four initialisations). With
the fast channels standardized by the statistics of the training discharges, the
skill over the causal GP is $+0.068$ $[+0.014, +0.112]$, $+0.094$ $[+0.067, +0.120]$,
$+0.141$ $[+0.106, +0.177]$ and $+0.101$ $[+0.061, +0.140]$ on the file-level splits
and $+0.103$ to $+0.112$ on the split by time, every interval excluding zero; in the
inclusive population it is $+0.098$ to $+0.165$ and $+0.097$ to $+0.121$ (the cut
values per split are in \S\ref{app:more}). This variant takes nothing from the discharge being
estimated except rows before the estimated one, and it is the estimator this paper
calls causal. (These numbers are from a first training of that estimator under a
30-epoch cap, one initialisation per split, with intervals that resample the
discharges only; the main text reports three initialisations under an 80-epoch cap,
with the initialisation drawn in the interval, and that is why the same point values
carry different verdicts there.) The whole-discharge variant is separated from it on one of the four file-level
cut splits ($+0.043$ $[+0.022, +0.066]$; the other three $-0.004$ to $+0.010$) and on
none of the initialisations of the split by time ($+0.003$ to $+0.015$): with failed

history-only network reads no fast channel, so its inputs are the same under either

pairing was made after the results were seen, and the causal variant was trained
under the 30-epoch cap while the history-only network had the 80-epoch cap.

\paragraph{Three supporting measurements.} (i) \emph{Memory is necessary.} A
memoryless regression (gradient-boosted trees) on the same 22 inputs of the target
row, including the last CES value, has a larger error: the recurrent estimator's skill
over it is $+0.156$ to $+0.231$ on the four cut splits and $+0.104$ to $+0.180$ on the
inclusive ones, every interval excluding zero, and that regression does not reach the
causal GP. A memoryless neural network on the same inputs (44{,}994 parameters),
registered with the variants above, reads the same way: $+0.167$ to $+0.196$ (cut)
and $+0.032$ to $+0.072$ (inclusive), every interval excluding zero, and on no cut
split is it above the causal GP. One row of fast channels plus one CES value is not
enough. (ii)
\emph{Inside a linear filter the fast-channel term is a measured increment.} A
20-parameter filter (\S\ref{sec:character}) is a CES-only filter plus a linear
fast-channel term; against the same filter refitted with the fast-channel gains set
to zero it has skill $+0.037$ to $+0.070$ on the four cut splits, every interval
excluding zero, and the increment is not resolved in the inclusive population. (iii)
\emph{The association exists at the level of increments.} Corrected for target noise,
the correlation between fast-channel increments and \Ti{} increments has a 95\% lower
bound of at least $+0.127$ at zero lag for every window between 10 and 200\,ms.

\paragraph{Dependence on the gap.} Resolved by the time since the last observed
value (a descriptive reading; \S\ref{app:more}), every estimator has a larger
error after a longer gap (\Ti{} RMSE of the recurrent estimator 168.8\,eV at 10\,ms
and 629.8\,eV beyond 50\,ms), and the skill over the causal GP keeps an interval
above zero up to 50\,ms in the cut population: $+0.115$ $[+0.085, +0.136]$ at 10\,ms, $+0.115$
$[+0.044, +0.176]$ at 20\,ms and $+0.072$ $[+0.008, +0.135]$ at 30--50\,ms; beyond
50\,ms it is $+0.109$ $[-0.009, +0.228]$ and the interval includes zero. In the
inclusive population the 20\,ms bin ($+0.088$ $[-0.014, +0.214]$) and the bin beyond
50\,ms ($-0.003$ $[-0.127, +0.088]$) include zero. 97.4\% of the scored \Ti{} rows of this readout (the earlier variant's)
are in the 10\,ms bin; the rows of the main batch differ (the four splits and the pooled
rows: \S\ref{sec:rows}).

\paragraph{What the fast channels are responding to.} The response of the trained
network to the fast channels is a same-instant co-movement: its Jacobian is a
high-pass kernel of about 100\,ms (the current value minus the mean of the preceding
ten steps), flat to about two standard deviations and reduced beyond three
(\S\ref{sec:twosources}).
Truncating the network's context costs $-0.066$ in skill at 20\,ms and saturates at
50\,ms. The autocorrelation $1/e$ times of \Ti{} (159\,ms), BES (161\,ms) and ECEI
(147\,ms) are close, which a common local evolution would produce and so would the
time scale of the discharge scenario. The
physical mechanism is not identified: per-discharge standardization removes the
absolute $T_e$ and $n_e$ that a collisional-exchange term would need, and
the 10\,ms files carry no beam-power or D-alpha signal with which events could be
labelled; the raw stream of twelve discharges gives a partial answer
(\S\ref{sec:bursts}).

\paragraph{Is the fast-channel increment a beam effect?} BES is the emission of the
heating beam, so its level follows the beam as well as the plasma, and a CES fit
depends on the beam. The files record the blips (\S\ref{sec:data:kstar}) but not the
beam power between them, so the two cannot be separated in general; what can be
read, after the fact and on the same rows, is where the share sits. By quintile of
the BES level of the row relative to its discharge median, the skill of the network
over its history-only twin at the natural cadence is largest in the lowest and in the
highest quintile, where the hold error is also largest: the lowest quintile carries
0.24 to 0.41 of the summed gain and the highest 0.33 to 0.52, and the interval
excludes zero in the lowest quintile on four of four splits and in the highest on
three of four. The share is therefore not confined to rows of low beam emission.
With CES thinned to 50\,ms it is above zero on four of four splits in the quintile of
rows whose BES level changed least from the previous row ($+0.070$ to $+0.174$; a
median change of 0.5\% of the discharge median). The ten rows after a blip, in which
the beam emission and \Ti{} are both returning to their levels (\S\ref{sec:bursts}),
are 0.055 to 0.060 of the scored rows; without them the share is $+0.058$ to $+0.105$
at the natural cadence and $+0.061$ to $+0.151$ at 50\,ms (four of four splits each;
causal variant $+0.026$ to $+0.107$ and $+0.047$ to $+0.149$, four of four), and
inside them it is $+0.086$ to $+0.184$ and $+0.144$ to $+0.252$ (four of four). This
does not identify the mechanism; it bounds one reading of it.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to suspicious rows.} The same rows were read again, after the
fact, leaving out those in which the scored value or the last visible sample is below
150\,eV or above 2.5\,keV, those with a jump of more than 1\,keV between the two, and
those within 30\,ms after a row whose BES level is below half of the discharge median
(a beam blip). With all four exclusions (0.885 to 0.898 of the rows kept) the
fast-channel increment at the natural cadence is $+0.084$ to $+0.145$ for the network and
$+0.044$ to $+0.152$ for the causal variant, and the margins over the causal GP are
$+0.084$ to $+0.136$ and $+0.069$ to $+0.126$, every interval excluding zero on four
of four splits. At 50\,ms the fast-channel increment stays ($+0.084$ to $+0.156$ on three
of four splits; causal variant $+0.084$ to $+0.147$ on four of four), and the margin
over the causal GP shrinks to $+0.038$ to $+0.087$ (three of four), most of the loss
coming from the rows with a jump of more than 1\,keV. The fast-channel increment thus
does not rest on the rows that are suspected first; the margin over the causal GP at
a sparse cadence partly does. In the population that keeps values above 3\,keV the
readouts are not stable under these exclusions.

\subsection{Registration history and order of events}
\label{app:registration}

\paragraph{KSTAR.} Test files were reserved before the architecture was chosen. The
adoption of the full-grid recurrent design and of per-discharge standardization used
results on the same four test splits, and the repeated cross-validation re-partitions
the same discharges. The form of the 20-parameter filter was read from the response of
the trained network on those test rows. The rotation scoring rule was set after a
first analysis (\S\ref{app:vrotrule}). The regime number of the KSTAR chord,
the gap-resolved reading and the example traces were computed for this paper and were
not registered. The per-discharge relation between the regime number and the
estimator ordering was registered, with both predictions and a minimum of 30 scored
rows per discharge, after the distribution of the regime number was known and before
any per-discharge skill was computed. The history-only network was registered with
its decision rule before it was trained; its training batch was interrupted and
resumed from saved runs. The rerun under an 80-epoch cap was registered, with the
same decision rule and a numerical expectation (a smaller increment, $+0.03$ to
$+0.10$), after the first result had been seen; the expectation of a smaller
increment did not hold. The analysis script of the rerun at first stopped on a check
of a logged field whose format had changed since the original network was trained;
the check was corrected before any number was read. The variants without
whole-discharge standardization and the memoryless network were registered, with
their rules and numerical expectations, in the same document as the history-only
network and before any of them was trained; of the expectations, the ranges for the
causal variant on the file-level splits and for the memoryless network were met, and
the expected loss of the causal variant on the split by time ($-0.12$ to $-0.02$
against the whole-discharge variant) was not: it is $-0.015$ to $-0.003$. The third panel of
Fig.~\ref{fig:trace} of the main text was added after the first two had been seen. The statements of
\S\ref{sec:twosources} (two on the first row after a blip, one on rows at bursts,
two on MAST) were registered before those readouts; of 15 numerical expectations 5
were met. The second stage of \S\ref{sec:character} was registered twice: fitted on half of the
test discharges, where three of its five statements failed, and then, with the same
models and bars, fitted on validation discharges; the comparison with a linear
combination and the inclusive population were registered after that result. The second
stage under the pooled protocol (\S\ref{sec:twostage}) was registered after those results,
with six statements, the rule that made the two-stage estimator the estimator of the main
text and numerical expectations; its handling of rows with missing window inputs was fixed
in an addendum before any run. Of the six numerical expectations four were met; the
fast-channel increment after the second stage and the share within 20\% came out above
the expected ranges.

\paragraph{MAST.} In order: (i) a pilot of 13 shots was inspected and those shots were
excluded; (ii) a development list of 300 shots, the fit rule, the tuning/test
division, the estimators and two decision rules were committed before any value of
the 300 shots was read, and the analysis was run (\S\ref{app:mast}); a second
run with wider tuning grids was registered because several optima sat on a grid edge.
During the first run the stored frame times of a few shots were found to be irregular
or non-increasing, and the nominal 5\,ms grid was adopted after a first test-set table
had been seen; both decisions were the same before and after. (iii) Breakdowns of the
development set by campaign, heating, coil state, array layout and normalized radius
were made after its result was known. (iv) The radial dependence read from those
breakdowns was registered as a hypothesis, with bins, two primary and four secondary
statements, decision rules and numerical expectations, before any value of the
remaining shots was read, and was computed once (\S\ref{sec:regime}). Of 22 numerical
expectations 13 were met; the directions held, several magnitudes did not.
